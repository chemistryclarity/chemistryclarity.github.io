## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Molarity.</Q>
<Answer>Moles of solute per litre of **solution**; unit mol/L (M).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Volume of water vs volume of solution.</Q>
<Answer>Dissolving the solid changes the total volume, so the solution is not exactly 250.0 mL. Dissolve the solid in less water, then add water **up to** a final volume of 250.0 mL (for example in a volumetric flask).</Answer>

## B. Calculating molarity and amounts

<Q n={3}>10.0 g NaOH in 500.0 mL.</Q>
<Answer>n = 10.0 g ÷ 40.00 g/mol = 0.250 mol. Molarity = 0.250 mol ÷ 0.5000 L = **0.500 M** (mol/L)</Answer>

<Q n={4}>KCl for 250.0 mL of 0.150 M.</Q>
<Answer>n = 0.150 mol/L × 0.2500 L = 0.03750 mol. M(KCl) = 74.55 g/mol, so mass = 0.03750 mol × 74.55 g/mol = **2.80 g**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Moles in 35.0 mL of 0.200 M.</Q>
<Answer>n = 0.200 mol/L × 0.0350 L = **0.00700 mol**</Answer>

## C. Dilution

<Q n={6}>Volume of 12.0 M HCl needed.</Q>
<Answer>$V_1 = \dfrac{M_2V_2}{M_1} = \dfrac{(0.600\ \text{M})(500.0\ \text{mL})}{12.0\ \text{M}} =$ **25.0 mL** (the units of M cancel)</Answer>

<Q n={7}>New concentration.</Q>
<Answer>$M_2 = \dfrac{M_1V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{(2.00\ \text{M})(50.0\ \text{mL})}{400.0\ \text{mL}} =$ **0.250 M** (the units of mL cancel)</Answer>

## D. Titrations

<Q n={8}>Concentration of HCl.</Q>
<Answer>n(NaOH) = 0.120 mol/L × 0.02250 L = 0.002700 mol = n(HCl) (1 : 1). [HCl] = 0.002700 mol ÷ 0.02500 L = **0.108 M**</Answer>

<Q n={9}>Concentration of H₂SO₄.</Q>
<Answer>n(NaOH) = 0.200 mol/L × 0.01875 L = 0.003750 mol. n(H₂SO₄) = 0.003750 mol NaOH × (1 mol H₂SO₄ / 2 mol NaOH) = 0.001875 mol. [H₂SO₄] = 0.001875 mol ÷ 0.01500 L = **0.125 M**</Answer>
