## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Ionic vs covalent.</Q>
<Answer>**Ionic:** electrons are transferred from one atom (usually a metal) to another (usually a non-metal), and the oppositely charged ions attract. **Covalent:** two atoms (usually non-metals) share one or more pairs of electrons.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Electronegativity.</Q>
<Answer>How strongly an atom attracts the shared pair of electrons in a covalent bond.</Answer>

## B. Classifying bonds

<Q n={3}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Cl–Cl: 0.00, **nonpolar covalent** (b) C–Cl: 3.16 − 2.55 = 0.61, **polar covalent** (c) N–H: 3.04 − 2.20 = 0.84, **polar covalent** (d) Mg–O: 3.44 − 1.31 = 2.13, **ionic** (e) Li–F: 3.98 − 0.98 = 3.00, **ionic**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Partial negative charge.</Q>
<Answer>C–Cl: **Cl** is δ−. N–H: **N** is δ−.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Increasing polarity.</Q>
<Answer>ΔEN: C–H 0.35, H–Br 0.76, O–H 1.24. Order: **C–H (least polar), H–Br, O–H (most polar)**</Answer>

## C. Ionic formulas

<Q n={6}>Formulas.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{K+}$ and $\ce{O^2-}$: **$\ce{K2O}$** (b) $\ce{Ca^2+}$ and $\ce{Br-}$: **$\ce{CaBr2}$** (c) $\ce{Al^3+}$ and $\ce{N^3-}$: **$\ce{AlN}$** (d) $\ce{Mg^2+}$ and $\ce{P^3-}$: **$\ce{Mg3P2}$**</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Ion formation.</Q>
<Answer>$\ce{Mg -> Mg^2+ + 2e-}$ and $\ce{Cl2 + 2e- -> 2Cl-}$. Overall the compound is $\ce{MgCl2}$.</Answer>

## D. Properties

<Q n={8}>Melting points.</Q>
<Answer>NaCl is an ionic lattice: melting requires overcoming many **strong attractions between oppositely charged ions**, which takes a lot of energy. Methane is made of separate molecules: melting only overcomes the **weak attractions between molecules**. The covalent C–H bonds do not break.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Conductivity.</Q>
<Answer>Conduction needs **charged particles that can move**. In the solid, the ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice. When molten or dissolved, the ions are free to move, so they can carry charge.</Answer>
