## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Equivalence point vs end point.</Q>
<Answer>The **equivalence point** is where the moles of base added exactly match the moles of acid (from the balanced equation). The **end point** is where the indicator changes colour. A good indicator makes them almost the same.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Equivalence-point pH.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Equal to 7**: strong acid + strong base. (b) **Above 7**: weak acid + strong base (formate is weakly basic). (c) **Below 7**: strong acid + weak base ($\ce{NH4+}$ is weakly acidic).</Answer>

## B. Strong acid with strong base

<Q n={3}>Equivalence volume.</Q>
<Answer>Moles HCl = 0.150 × 0.02000 = 0.00300 mol. Volume NaOH = 0.00300 ÷ 0.100 = **30.0 mL**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Initial pH.</Q>
<Answer>pH = −log(0.150) = **0.82**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>After 10.00 mL NaOH.</Q>
<Answer>NaOH added = 0.00100 mol; HCl left = 0.00300 − 0.00100 = 0.00200 mol in 30.00 mL. [H₃O⁺] = 0.0667 M; pH = **1.18**</Answer>

<Q n={6}>After 40.00 mL NaOH.</Q>
<Answer>NaOH added = 0.00400 mol; excess OH⁻ = 0.00400 − 0.00300 = 0.00100 mol in 60.00 mL. [OH⁻] = 0.0167 M; pOH = 1.78; pH = **12.22**</Answer>

## C. Weak acids and indicators

<Q n={7}>pKa and Ka from the half-equivalence point.</Q>
<Answer>At half-equivalence, pH = pKa, so **pKa = 4.20** and $K_\text{a} = 10^{-4.20} =$ **6.3 × 10⁻⁵**</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Indicator choice.</Q>
<Answer>**Phenolphthalein** (pH 8.2–10.0). The equivalence point of a weak acid–strong base titration is above 7, inside the steep jump where phenolphthalein changes colour. Methyl orange would change far too early.</Answer>
