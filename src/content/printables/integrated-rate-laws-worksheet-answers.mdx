## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Half-life:** the time for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its value. (b) **Elementary step:** a single step in a mechanism, whose rate law follows from the particles that collide. (c) **Rate-determining step:** the **slowest** step, which sets the overall rate. (d) **Intermediate:** a species **made** in one step and **used up** in a later one.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Plots and units.</Q>
<Answer>**Zero:** [A] against t, slope −k, k in **M s⁻¹**. **First:** ln[A] against t, slope −k, k in **s⁻¹**. **Second:** 1/[A] against t, slope +k, k in **M⁻¹ s⁻¹**.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>First-order time.</Q>
<Answer>

$t = \dfrac{\ln([\ce{A}]_0/[\ce{A}])}{k} = \dfrac{\ln(0.800\ \text{M}/0.200\ \text{M})}{0.0200\ \text{min}^{-1}} = \dfrac{1.386}{0.0200\ \text{min}^{-1}} =$ **69.3 min** (exactly two half-lives of 34.7 min)

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Second order.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{1}{(0.150\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1})(0.0500\ \text{M})} =$ **133 s**

(b) $\dfrac{1}{[\ce{A}]} = \dfrac{1}{0.0500\ \text{M}} + (0.150\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1})(200.\ \text{s}) = 20.0\ \text{M}^{-1} + 30.0\ \text{M}^{-1} = 50.0\ \text{M}^{-1}$, so $[\ce{A}] =$ **0.0200 M**

</Answer>

## C. Order from data

<Q n={7}>First order from data.</Q>
<Answer>

In each 100 s interval [A] is multiplied by the **same fraction**: $\dfrac{0.300}{0.400} = 0.750$, $\dfrac{0.225}{0.300} = 0.750$, $\dfrac{0.169}{0.225} = 0.751$. A constant fraction in equal times means **first order** (ln[A] against t is a straight line).

$k = \dfrac{\ln(0.400\ \text{M}/0.300\ \text{M})}{100\ \text{s}} = \dfrac{0.2877}{100\ \text{s}} =$ **2.88 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹**

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Successive half-lives.</Q>
<Answer>

First: $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{1}{(0.0500\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1})(0.100\ \text{M})} =$ **200. s**. Second: $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{1}{(0.0500\ \text{M}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1})(0.0500\ \text{M})} =$ **400. s**

Each half-life is **twice** the one before. If measured half-lives keep doubling, the reaction is second order (first order gives equal half-lives).

</Answer>
