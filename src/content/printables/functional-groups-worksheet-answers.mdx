## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Functional groups.</Q>
<Answer>(a) An **atom or group of atoms** that gives a family of organic compounds its typical reactions and properties. (b) Both contain the **same functional group (–OH)**; reactions happen at the functional group, and the different chain length has little effect.</Answer>

## C. Isomers and properties

<Q n={5}>Functional group isomers.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **propanal**, $\ce{CH3CH2CHO}$ (aldehyde), and **propanone**, $\ce{CH3COCH3}$ (ketone). (b) **ethanol**, $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ (alcohol), and **methoxymethane**, $\ce{CH3OCH3}$ (ether).</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Boiling points.</Q>
<Answer>**Propane** has only weak dispersion forces. **Methoxymethane** is polar (C–O bonds), so it has dipole–dipole forces too, but no O–H bond, so no hydrogen bonding. **Ethanol** has an O–H group and forms **hydrogen bonds** between molecules, so much more energy is needed to separate them. **Ethanoic acid** has an O–H and a C=O group, forms **stronger and more hydrogen bonds** (molecules pair up), and is also heavier, so it boils highest.</Answer>

## D. Esters

<Q n={7}>Esters.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **methyl ethanoate**: $\ce{CH3COOH + CH3OH <=> CH3COOCH3 + H2O}$ (b) **ethyl propanoate**, $\ce{CH3CH2COOCH2CH3}$ (c) **methanoic acid** ($\ce{HCOOH}$) and **propan-1-ol** ($\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH}$)</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Yield of methyl ethanoate.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $n(\ce{CH3OH}) = \dfrac{4.00\ \text{g}}{32.04\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.1248\ \text{mol}$; the ratio is 1 : 1, so $n(\text{ester}) = 0.1248\ \text{mol}$

$m = 0.1248\ \text{mol} \times 74.08\ \text{g/mol} =$ **9.25 g**

(b) Percent yield $= \dfrac{6.50\ \text{g}}{9.25\ \text{g}} \times 100\ \% =$ **70.3 %**. The reaction is **reversible** and reaches equilibrium, so not all the methanol is converted (some product is also lost when it is separated and purified).

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Identifying the liquid.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) In 100 g: $n(\ce{C}) = \dfrac{54.53\ \text{g}}{12.01\ \text{g/mol}} = 4.540\ \text{mol}$; $n(\ce{H}) = \dfrac{9.15\ \text{g}}{1.008\ \text{g/mol}} = 9.077\ \text{mol}$; $n(\ce{O}) = \dfrac{36.32\ \text{g}}{16.00\ \text{g/mol}} = 2.270\ \text{mol}$

Divide by the smallest (2.270 mol): C 2.000, H 3.999, O 1, so the empirical formula is **$\ce{C2H4O}$** (44.05 g/mol).

$\dfrac{88.1\ \text{g/mol}}{44.05\ \text{g/mol}} = 2.00$, so the molecular formula is **$\ce{C4H8O2}$**.

(b) $\ce{C4H8O2}$ with a sweet smell and no reaction with carbonate: an **ester**, for example **ethyl ethanoate**, $\ce{CH3COOCH2CH3}$ (methyl propanoate and propyl methanoate are also correct).

</Answer>
