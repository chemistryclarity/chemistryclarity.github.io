## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Effective nuclear charge.</Q>
<Answer>The net positive charge felt by an outer electron after shielding by the inner electrons. (a) Na: 11 − 10 = **1** (b) S: 16 − 10 = **6**</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Radius across a period.</Q>
<Answer>Across a period protons are added, but the electrons go into the same outer shell and the number of inner (shielding) electrons stays the same. $Z_\text{eff}$ increases, so the outer shell is pulled closer to the nucleus.</Answer>

## B. Ranking

<Q n={3}>Atomic radius.</Q>
<Answer>In period 3, radius decreases from left to right (S < Mg < Na); Ca is below Mg with an extra shell. **S < Mg < Na < Ca**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>First ionization energy.</Q>
<Answer>Down group 1 ionization energy decreases (K < Na < Li); Ne, a noble gas at the right of period 2, is highest. **K < Na < Li < Ne**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Electronegativity.</Q>
<Answer>**Na (0.93) < I (2.66) < Br (2.96) < Cl (3.16)**</Answer>

## C. Ionization energy

<Q n={6}>Group 1.</Q>
<Answer>Ionization energy **decreases** down the group, from 520.2 kJ/mol (Li) to 375.7 kJ/mol (Cs). Each element has one more shell, so the outer s electron is farther from the nucleus and more shielded by inner electrons. It is held less strongly and is easier to remove.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Period 2 exceptions.</Q>
<Answer>**B < Be:** boron's outer electron is in a 2p orbital, which is higher in energy than beryllium's 2s orbital, so it is easier to remove. **O < N:** nitrogen's three 2p electrons are unpaired; oxygen's fourth 2p electron must pair with another, and the repulsion between the paired electrons makes one of them easier to remove.</Answer>

## D. Ionic radius

<Q n={8}>Ions and atoms.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **$\ce{Cl-}$** is larger: the extra electron increases repulsion between electrons, and the same 17 protons now hold 18 electrons. (b) **Mg** is larger: $\ce{Mg^2+}$ has lost its whole third shell (3s²), and its remaining 10 electrons are held by 12 protons.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Isoelectronic series.</Q>
<Answer>**$\ce{S^2-} > \ce{Cl-} > \ce{K+} > \ce{Ca^2+}$**. All have 18 electrons, but the number of protons increases (16, 17, 19, 20). More protons pull the same number of electrons in more tightly, so the ion is smaller.</Answer>
