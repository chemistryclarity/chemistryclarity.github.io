## A. Batteries and fuel cells

<Q n={1}>Batteries.</Q>
<Answer>(a) A **primary** battery is used once; its reaction cannot practically be reversed. A **secondary** battery can be recharged. (b) An external current forces electrons in the opposite direction, driving the cell reaction **backwards** (electrolysis): Li⁺ ions move back into the graphite electrode, restoring the reactants.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Fuel cell.</Q>
<Answer>

Anode: $\ce{2H2 -> 4H+ + 4e-}$; cathode: $\ce{O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O}$; overall: $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$

$E°_\text{cell} = 1.23\ \text{V} - 0.00\ \text{V} = \textbf{1.23 V}$

Advantage: the only exhaust is **water** (and it doesn't run down while fuelled). Challenge: hydrogen is hard to **store** and is often made from fossil fuels.

</Answer>

## B. Corrosion

<Q n={3}>Three test tubes.</Q>
<Answer>Tube 1: nail in tap water open to air: **rusts**. Tube 2: nail in boiled water (oxygen removed) with a layer of oil on top: **no rust** (no oxygen). Tube 3: nail in dry air with a drying agent (anhydrous calcium chloride), stoppered: **no rust** (no water). Only the tube with both water and oxygen shows rust.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Rusting half-equations.</Q>
<Answer>

Anode: $\ce{Fe -> Fe^2+ + 2e-}$; cathode (neutral water): $\ce{O2 + 2H2O + 4e- -> 4OH-}$

In acid: $E°_\text{cell} = 1.23\ \text{V} - (-0.44\ \text{V}) = \textbf{1.67 V}$

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Coatings.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Zinc is **more reactive** than iron (E° −0.76 V versus −0.44 V), so where both are exposed, zinc is oxidized instead of iron: **sacrificial protection**. (b) Tin is **less reactive** than iron, so at a scratch the iron becomes the **anode** and corrodes, faster than if it were bare.</Answer>

## C. Calculations

<Q n={6}>AA cell.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $2.500\ \text{A h} \times 3600\ \text{s/h} = \textbf{9000 C}$

(b) $\dfrac{9000\ \text{C}}{96\,485\ \text{C/mol}} = \textbf{0.0933 mol e}^-$

(c) 2 mol e⁻ per mol Zn: $\dfrac{0.09328\ \text{mol}}{2} \times 65.38\ \text{g/mol} = \textbf{3.05 g Zn}$

(d) $9000\ \text{C} \times 1.5\ \text{V} = 1.35 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J}$, about **14 kJ**

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Hydrogen used.</Q>
<Answer>

Charge: $1.00\ \text{A} \times 3600\ \text{s} = 3600\ \text{C}$; electrons: $\dfrac{3600\ \text{C}}{96\,485\ \text{C/mol}} = 0.03731\ \text{mol}$

H₂ (2 e⁻ each): $0.01866\ \text{mol} \times 2.016\ \text{g/mol} = \textbf{0.0376 g}$

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Sacrificial anode.</Q>
<Answer>

Moles of Mg: $\dfrac{5000\ \text{g}}{24.31\ \text{g/mol}} = 205.7\ \text{mol}$; electrons: $2 \times 205.7 = 411.4\ \text{mol}$

Charge: $411.4\ \text{mol} \times 96\,485\ \text{C/mol} = 3.97 \times 10^{7}\ \text{C}$

Time: $\dfrac{3.97 \times 10^{7}\ \text{C}}{0.50\ \text{A}} = 7.94 \times 10^{7}\ \text{s}$; $\dfrac{7.94 \times 10^{7}\ \text{s}}{3.156 \times 10^{7}\ \text{s/year}} = \textbf{about 2.5 years}$

</Answer>
