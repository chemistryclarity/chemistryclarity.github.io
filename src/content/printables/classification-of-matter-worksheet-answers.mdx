## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Compound vs mixture.</Q>
<Answer>A **compound** contains two or more elements **chemically bonded** in a **fixed ratio** (e.g. water, $\ce{H2O}$); it has new properties and can be broken down only by chemical reactions. A **mixture** contains substances that are **not chemically joined**, its composition can vary (e.g. salt water), and it can be separated by physical methods.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Why $\ce{O2}$ is an element.</Q>
<Answer>Each $\ce{O2}$ molecule contains **only one kind of atom** (oxygen). A compound must contain at least two different elements.</Answer>

## B. Classifying matter

<Q n={3}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>(a) gold: **element** (b) NaCl: **compound** (c) air: **homogeneous mixture** (d) muddy water: **heterogeneous mixture** (e) sugar in tea: **homogeneous mixture** (f) CO: **compound**</Answer>

## C. Physical and chemical changes

<Q n={4}>Physical or chemical.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **physical**: change of state only (b) **chemical**: new substances (lactic acid) form; smell and texture change (c) **chemical**: wax burns to form carbon dioxide and water, releasing heat and light (the wax also melts, a physical change) (d) **physical**: still wood (e) **physical**: sugar is unchanged and can be recovered by evaporation</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Properties.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **physical** (b) **chemical** (c) **physical** (d) **chemical**</Answer>

## D. Separating mixtures

<Q n={6}>Methods.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **filtration** (b) **evaporation** (crystallisation) (c) **distillation** (d) **chromatography** (e) **magnetism** (a magnet attracts the iron)</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Sand, salt and water.</Q>
<Answer>1. **Filter** the mixture: sand stays in the filter paper; salt water passes through. 2. **Wash** the sand with a little water and let it dry. 3. **Evaporate** the water from the filtrate (or heat gently until crystals form) to leave dry salt.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Mixture vs compound FeS.</Q>
<Answer>Any two of: in the **mixture**, a magnet can pull out the iron, but not from FeS; the mixture can have **any ratio** of iron to sulfur, while FeS has a **fixed ratio** (1 Fe : 1 S atoms; about 63.5% Fe by mass); making FeS needs a **chemical reaction** (heating, which releases heat), while making the mixture does not; FeS has **different properties** from both iron and sulfur.</Answer>

<Q n={9}>River water.</Q>
<Answer>A pure substance has a fixed boiling point at a given pressure. The river water boils 0.4 °C higher than pure water at the same pressure, so it is **not pure**: it is a **mixture** containing dissolved substances (for example salts).</Answer>
