## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>2.5 acidic; 7.0 neutral; 9.8 basic; 6.9 acidic (just below 7).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>pH 2 vs pH 5.</Q>
<Answer>A difference of 3 pH units = $10^3$ = **1000 times** greater.</Answer>

## B. Strong acids

<Q n={3}>0.0010 M HCl.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{H3O+}] = 0.0010$ M; pH = −log(0.0010) = **3.00**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>0.25 M HNO₃.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{H3O+}] = 0.25$ M; pH = −log(0.25) = **0.60**</Answer>

## C. Strong bases

<Q n={5}>0.010 M NaOH.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{OH-}] = 0.010$ M; pOH = 2.00; pH = 14.00 − 2.00 = **12.00**</Answer>

<Q n={6}>0.0050 M Ba(OH)₂.</Q>
<Answer>Two $\ce{OH-}$ per formula unit: $[\ce{OH-}] = 0.010$ M; pOH = 2.00; pH = **12.00**</Answer>

## D. Concentration from pH

<Q n={7}>[H₃O⁺] from pH.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $10^{-3.20} =$ **6.3 × 10⁻⁴ M** (b) $10^{-11.50} =$ **3.2 × 10⁻¹² M**</Answer>

<Q n={8}>[OH⁻] at pH 5.00.</Q>
<Answer>pOH = 14.00 − 5.00 = 9.00, so $[\ce{OH-}] = 10^{-9.00} =$ **1.0 × 10⁻⁹ M**</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>100-fold dilution of 0.10 M HCl.</Q>
<Answer>New concentration = 0.10 ÷ 100 = 0.0010 M, so pH = **3.00**. The pH rises by **2 units** (from 1.00), one unit for each factor of 10.</Answer>
