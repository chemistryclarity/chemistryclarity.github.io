## A. Definitions

<Q n={1}>Lattice enthalpy.</Q>
<Answer>(a) The enthalpy change when 1 mol of an ionic solid forms from its gaseous ions under standard conditions. (b) Lattice: $\ce{K+(g) + Br-(g) -> KBr(s)}$. Formation: $\ce{K(s) + 1/2 Br2(l) -> KBr(s)}$ (bromine is a liquid in its standard state).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Endothermic or exothermic.</Q>
<Answer>(a) endothermic (b) **exothermic** (c) **endothermic** (d) endothermic (e) exothermic</Answer>

## B. Calculations

<Q n={3}>LiF.</Q>
<Answer>

Steps to the gaseous ions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&159.3 + 520.2 + 79.4 - 328.2\ (\text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= +430.7\ \text{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H_\text{latt}° \\[2pt]
&= -616.0\ \text{kJ/mol} - 430.7\ \text{kJ/mol} \\[2pt]
&= \boldsymbol{-1046.7}\ \textbf{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>EA of bromine.</Q>
<Answer>

$\Delta H_\text{f}° = \Delta H_\text{sub}(\ce{K}) + \text{IE}_1 + \Delta H_\text{atom}(\ce{Br}) + \text{EA} + \Delta H_\text{latt}°$, so:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{EA} &= -393.8 - 89.0 - 418.8 \\[2pt]
&\quad - 111.9 - (-689.0)\ (\text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= \boldsymbol{-324.5}\ \textbf{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

## C. Trends

<Q n={5}>Order.</Q>
<Answer>**KCl, NaCl, CaO, MgO** (least to most negative). KCl and NaCl have 1+ and 1− ions; $\ce{K+}$ is larger than $\ce{Na+}$, so KCl is less negative. CaO and MgO have 2+ and 2− ions, so their attractions are about four times stronger; $\ce{Mg^2+}$ is smaller than $\ce{Ca^2+}$, so MgO is the most negative.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Second electron affinity of oxygen.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Steps other than EA₂:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&177.8 + 589.8 + 1145.4 \\[2pt]
&\quad + 249.2 - 141.0\ (\text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= +2021.2\ \text{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{EA}_2 &= -634.9 - 2021.2 \\[2pt]
&\quad - (-3414)\ (\text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= \boldsymbol{+758}\ \textbf{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b) It is **endothermic**: the electron is added to $\ce{O-}$, which is already negative, so it is repelled and energy must be put in.

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={7}>Hypothetical MgCl.</Q>
<Answer>

(a)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H_\text{f}° &= 147.1 + 737.7 + 121.3 \\[2pt]
&\quad - 348.6 - 753.0\ (\text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= \boldsymbol{-95.5}\ \textbf{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b) Products minus reactants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H &= (1\ \text{mol})(-641.3\ \text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&\quad - (2\ \text{mol})(-95.5\ \text{kJ/mol}) \\[2pt]
&= \boldsymbol{-450.3}\ \textbf{kJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

(c) MgCl would be stable relative to the elements, but it would release a lot of energy by turning into Mg and $\ce{MgCl2}$. Removing the second electron costs 1450.7 kJ/mol, but the much more negative lattice enthalpy of $\ce{MgCl2}$ (2+ ions) more than repays it, so $\ce{MgCl2}$ forms.

</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Ionic model.</Q>
<Answer>NaCl is almost purely **ionic**: the model of separate spherical ions describes it well. In AgI, the large, easily polarized $\ce{I-}$ ion is distorted by $\ce{Ag+}$, so some electron density is shared between them: the bonding has **covalent character**, which adds extra attraction not included in the ionic model.</Answer>
