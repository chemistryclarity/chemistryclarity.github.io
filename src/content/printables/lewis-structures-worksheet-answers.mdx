## A. Counting electrons

<Q n={1}>Valence electrons.</Q>
<Answer>(a) 4 + 4 × 1 = **8** (b) 5 + 3 × 7 = **26** (c) 5 + 4 × 1 − 1 = **8** (d) 4 + 3 × 6 + 2 = **24**</Answer>

## B. Drawing structures

<Q n={2}>H₂S, NF₃, CCl₄.</Q>
<Answer>(a) 8 e⁻: H–S–H, **2 lone pairs on S** (b) 26 e⁻: N bonded to three F, **1 lone pair on N**, 3 lone pairs on each F (c) 32 e⁻: C bonded to four Cl, no lone pairs on C, **3 lone pairs on each Cl**</Answer>

<Q n={3}>CH₂O and C₂H₄.</Q>
<Answer>(a) 12 e⁻: C single-bonded to two H and **double-bonded to O**; 2 lone pairs on O. (b) 12 e⁻: **C=C double bond**, each C single-bonded to two H; no lone pairs.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>CS₂.</Q>
<Answer>16 e⁻ (like $\ce{CO2}$): **S=C=S**, with 2 lone pairs on each S: **4 lone pairs** in total.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>NH₄⁺.</Q>
<Answer>8 e⁻: N bonded to four H, no lone pairs, drawn in square brackets with a + charge. Formal charge on N = 5 − 0 − ½(8) = **+1** (equal to the ion's charge).</Answer>

## C. Formal charge and resonance

<Q n={6}>Ozone.</Q>
<Answer>O=O–O: the central O has 1 lone pair, the double-bonded O has 2 lone pairs and the single-bonded O has 3 lone pairs. Formal charges: double-bonded O 6 − 4 − 2 = **0**; central O 6 − 2 − 3 = **+1**; single-bonded O 6 − 6 − 1 = **−1**. They add up to 0, the charge of the molecule.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Nitrate.</Q>
<Answer>24 e⁻: N in the centre with **one N=O double bond and two N–O single bonds**. The three resonance structures differ only in which O has the double bond; they are linked by ↔ and drawn in brackets with a − charge. Formal charges: N +1, each single-bonded O −1, double-bonded O 0. Because the real ion is a hybrid, **all three N–O bonds are the same length**, between a single and a double bond.</Answer>

## D. Exceptions

<Q n={8}>BF₃.</Q>
<Answer>Boron has only 3 valence electrons and forms three single B–F bonds, so it is surrounded by only **6 electrons**, an incomplete octet.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>XeF₄.</Q>
<Answer>Xe is bonded to four F, each F with 3 lone pairs (8 + 24 = 32 e⁻). The remaining 4 e⁻ go on Xe as **2 lone pairs**. Xenon is surrounded by 8 + 4 = **12 electrons**, an expanded octet. This is possible because xenon is a period-5 element, large enough to hold more than 8 electrons in its valence shell.</Answer>
