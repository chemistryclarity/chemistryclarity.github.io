## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Ka expression.</Q>
<Answer>$K_\text{a} = \dfrac{[\ce{H3O+}][\ce{A-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$. A large $K_\text{a}$ means the acid ionizes to a greater extent: it is a stronger acid.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Ranking and pKa of HF.</Q>
<Answer>Strongest to weakest: **HF > acetic acid > HCN** (largest to smallest Ka). pKa(HF) = −log(6.8 × 10⁻⁴) = **3.17**</Answer>

## B. pH of weak acids

<Q n={3}>0.20 M acetic acid.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{H3O+}] \approx \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{-5} \times 0.20} = 1.9 \times 10^{-3}$ M (about 0.9% of 0.20 M, so the shortcut is valid). pH = **2.72**; percent ionization ≈ **0.9%**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>0.25 M HCN.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{H3O+}] \approx \sqrt{4.9 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.25} = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$ M. pH = **4.96**</Answer>

## C. Ka from a measured pH

<Q n={5}>0.050 M acid, pH 3.20.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{H3O+}] = [\ce{A-}] = 10^{-3.20} = 6.3 \times 10^{-4}$ M; [HA] = 0.050 M − 0.00063 M = 0.0494 M. $K_\text{a} = (6.3 \times 10^{-4})^2 \div 0.0494 =$ **8.1 × 10⁻⁶**; percent ionization = **1.3%**</Answer>

## D. Weak bases

<Q n={6}>0.20 M ammonia.</Q>
<Answer>$[\ce{OH-}] \approx \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{-5} \times 0.20} = 1.9 \times 10^{-3}$ M; pOH = 2.72; pH = **11.28**</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Kb of acetate.</Q>
<Answer>$K_\text{b} = K_\text{w} / K_\text{a} = 1.0 \times 10^{-14} \div 1.8 \times 10^{-5} =$ **5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰**</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Why the pH values differ.</Q>
<Answer>HCl is a **strong** acid: it ionizes completely, so [H₃O⁺] = 0.20 M and pH = 0.70. Acetic acid is **weak**: less than 1% ionizes, so [H₃O⁺] is only 1.9 × 10⁻³ M and pH = 2.72.</Answer>
