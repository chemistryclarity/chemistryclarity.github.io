## A. Definitions

<Q n={1}>Brønsted–Lowry acid and base.</Q>
<Answer>An acid is a proton ($\ce{H+}$) **donor**; a base is a proton ($\ce{H+}$) **acceptor**.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Strong vs concentrated.</Q>
<Answer>**Strong** means the acid ionizes completely in water. **Concentrated** means a large amount of acid is dissolved per volume. A dilute solution of a strong acid and a concentrated solution of a weak acid are both possible.</Answer>

## B. Acids, bases and conjugate pairs

<Q n={3}>Identify acid and base.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Acid: $\ce{HNO3}$; base: $\ce{H2O}$

(b) Acid: $\ce{HCl}$; base: $\ce{NH3}$

(c) Acid: $\ce{HCO3-}$; base: $\ce{OH-}$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Conjugate bases.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{F-}$ (b) $\ce{HSO4-}$ (c) $\ce{NH3}$ (d) $\ce{OH-}$</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Conjugate acids.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{NH4+}$ (b) $\ce{H2O}$ (c) $\ce{H3O+}$ (d) $\ce{HCO3-}$</Answer>

## C. Strong and weak

<Q n={6}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>$\ce{HCl}$ and $\ce{HNO3}$: strong acids. $\ce{CH3COOH}$: weak acid. $\ce{NaOH}$: strong base. $\ce{NH3}$: weak base.</Answer>

## D. Neutralization

<Q n={7}>Neutralization equations.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{HCl + KOH -> KCl + H2O}$

(b) $\ce{H2SO4 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO4 + 2H2O}$

(c) $\ce{2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 -> Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O}$

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Water acting both ways.</Q>
<Answer>Such a substance is **amphoteric**. Water can accept a proton (becoming $\ce{H3O+}$) when it meets a stronger acid, and donate a proton (becoming $\ce{OH-}$) when it meets a base such as ammonia.</Answer>
