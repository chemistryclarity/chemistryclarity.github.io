## A. The quantum numbers

<Q n={1}>Meanings.</Q>
<Answer>**n**: the shell (size and main energy). **l**: the subshell (shape). **mₗ**: the orientation of the orbital in space. **mₛ**: the spin of the electron (+½ or −½).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>n = 4.</Q>
<Answer>(a) l = 0 (**4s**), 1 (**4p**), 2 (**4d**), 3 (**4f**). (b) Orbitals: 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = **16** = 4². Electrons: 2 × 16 = **32** = 2 × 4².</Answer>

## B. Allowed or not?

<Q n={3}>Allowed sets.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) **Allowed**: a 1s electron.

(b) **Not allowed**: for l = 1, mₗ can only be −1, 0 or +1.

(c) **Allowed**: a 3d electron.

(d) **Not allowed**: mₛ must be +½ or −½.

(e) **Not allowed**: l must be at most n − 1 = 2.

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Subshells.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **2p**, 6 electrons (b) **3d**, 10 (c) **5s**, 2 (d) **4f**, 14</Answer>

## C. Electrons in atoms

<Q n={5}>Possible sets.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) 1s¹: **(1, 0, 0, +½)** (or −½)

(b) Potassium is [Ar] 4s¹: **(4, 0, 0, +½)** (or −½)

(c) 2p: n = 2, l = 1, mₗ = −1, 0 or +1, mₛ = ±½, e.g. **(2, 1, 0, +½)**. (By Hund's rule, carbon's two 2p electrons are in different orbitals with the same spin.)

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Pauli.</Q>
<Answer>All electrons in one orbital share the same n, l and mₗ, so they can only differ in mₛ. mₛ has only two values (+½ and −½), so a third electron would have to repeat a full set of quantum numbers, which the Pauli exclusion principle forbids.</Answer>

## D. Shapes and nodes

<Q n={7}>Shapes and nodes.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) s: a sphere centred on the nucleus. $p_z$: two lobes along the z axis, on either side of the nucleus, with a nodal plane (the xy plane) between them.

(b) 2s: **0 angular, 1 radial**. 2p: **1 angular, 0 radial**. 3d: **2 angular, 0 radial**.

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Block widths.</Q>
<Answer>Each orbital holds 2 electrons, and each element in a block adds one more electron to that subshell. s has 1 orbital (2 electrons, **2 columns**), p has 3 (6 electrons, **6 columns**), d has 5 (10 electrons, **10 columns**) and f has 7 (14 electrons, **14 columns**). The numbers of orbitals come from mₗ having 2l + 1 values.</Answer>
