## A. Waves and photons

<Q n={1}>Order of energy.</Q>
<Answer>From lowest to highest energy: **radio waves → microwaves → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays**</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Microwaves.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) 12.2 cm = 0.122 m

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{0.122\ \text{m}} \\[4pt]
&= \boldsymbol{2.46 \times 10^{9}\ \textbf{s}^{-1}}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = h\nu = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J}\,\cancel{\text{s}} \times 2.457 \times 10^{9}\ \cancel{\text{s}^{-1}} \\[4pt]
&= \boldsymbol{1.63 \times 10^{-24}\ \textbf{J}}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Blue light.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{450 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{m}} \\[4pt]
&= \boldsymbol{4.41 \times 10^{-19}\ \textbf{J}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Per mole: $4.414 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1} = 2.66 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J/mol} = \textbf{266 kJ/mol}$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>UV and bond breaking.</Q>
<Answer>

(a)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{254 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{m}} \\[4pt]
&= \boldsymbol{7.82 \times 10^{-19}\ \textbf{J}}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b) Energy per bond:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = \frac{498 \times 10^{3}\ \text{J/mol}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}} = 8.270 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J} \\[4pt]
&\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{8.270 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}} \\[4pt]
&= 2.40 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} = \textbf{240 nm}
\end{aligned}
$$

Longer wavelengths carry less energy per photon, so 240 nm is the longest that works. (The 254 nm lamp is just too weak to break O=O.)

</Answer>

## B. Line spectra and the Bohr model

<Q n={5}>Spectra.</Q>
<Answer>A **continuous spectrum** contains all wavelengths (a hot solid). A **line spectrum** contains only certain exact wavelengths (an excited gas). Each line is a photon with energy equal to the gap between two levels; since only certain wavelengths appear, only certain energies are allowed: the levels are **quantized**.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Bohr calculations.</Q>
<Answer>

(a)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta E = 2.179 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J} \left(\tfrac{1}{2^2} - \tfrac{1}{4^2}\right) = 4.086 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J} \\[4pt]
&\lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{4.086 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}} \\[4pt]
&= 4.86 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} = \textbf{486 nm}\ \text{(visible, blue-green)}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta E = 2.179 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J} \left(\tfrac{1}{1^2} - \tfrac{1}{2^2}\right) = 1.634 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J} \\[4pt]
&\lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{1.634 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}} \\[4pt]
&= 1.22 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} = \textbf{122 nm}\ \text{(ultraviolet)}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Ionization from n = 2.</Q>
<Answer>

$E_2 = -\dfrac{2.179 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{J}}{2^2} = -5.448 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$, so the energy needed is $\boldsymbol{5.45 \times 10^{-19}\ \textbf{J}}$ per atom.

Per mole: $5.448 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1} = 3.28 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J/mol} = \textbf{328 kJ/mol}$

</Answer>

## C. Challenge

<Q n={8}>One photon or one mole?</Q>
<Answer>

182 kJ is the energy of a **mole** of photons ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$ photons), not of one photon. For one photon:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}}{656 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{m}} \\[4pt]
&= \boldsymbol{3.03 \times 10^{-19}\ \textbf{J}}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>
