## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) A compound of **carbon and hydrogen only**. (b) Containing **only single C–C bonds**, so each carbon holds the maximum number of hydrogen atoms (alkanes). (c) A family of compounds with the **same general formula** and similar chemistry, each member differing from the next by **$\ce{CH2}$**.</Answer>

## B. Formulas

<Q n={2}>Molecular formulas.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **$\ce{C6H14}$** (b) **$\ce{C10H22}$** (c) **$\ce{C4H8}$** (d) **$\ce{C3H4}$** (e) 2n + 2 = 20, so n = 9: **$\ce{C9H20}$**</Answer>

## C. Naming

<Q n={3}>Names.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **butane** (b) **2-methylpropane** (c) **2-methylpentane** (d) **2,2-dimethylbutane** (e) **2,3-dimethylbutane** (f) **3-ethylpentane**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Condensed formulas.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH3}$ (b) $\ce{(CH3)3CCH2CH(CH3)2}$ (c) $\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH(C2H5)CH2CH3}$ (d) $\ce{CH3CH=CHCH3}$</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Corrected names.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **pentane**: a methyl on carbon 1 just extends the chain (longest chain missed). (b) **2-methylbutane**: the ethyl group is part of a longer chain (longest chain missed). (c) **2-methylbutane**: numbered from the wrong end (lowest locants). (d) **2,2-dimethylbutane**: numbered from the wrong end.</Answer>

## D. Isomers and alkenes

<Q n={6}>Isomers of C₆H₁₄.</Q>
<Answer>**hexane** $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2CH2CH3}$; **2-methylpentane** $\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH3}$; **3-methylpentane** $\ce{CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3}$; **2,2-dimethylbutane** $\ce{CH3C(CH3)2CH2CH3}$; **2,3-dimethylbutane** $\ce{CH3CH(CH3)CH(CH3)CH3}$</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Alkenes.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **but-1-ene**, **but-2-ene**, **pent-1-ene**. (b) The chain must be numbered so the double bond gets the lowest locant; numbered from the other end, "but-3-ene" is **but-1-ene**.</Answer>

## E. Combustion

<Q n={8}>Burning octane.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{2C8H18 + 25O2 -> 16CO2 + 18H2O}$

(b) $n(\ce{C8H18}) = \dfrac{100.0\ \text{g}}{114.22\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.8755\ \text{mol}$

$n(\ce{CO2}) = 0.8755\ \text{mol}\ \ce{C8H18} \times \dfrac{16\ \text{mol}\ \ce{CO2}}{2\ \text{mol}\ \ce{C8H18}} = 7.004\ \text{mol}$

$m(\ce{CO2}) = 7.004\ \text{mol} \times 44.01\ \text{g/mol} =$ **308.2 g**

</Answer>

## F. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Formula from combustion data.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) All the C ends up in $\ce{CO2}$ and all the H in $\ce{H2O}$:

$n(\ce{C}) = n(\ce{CO2}) = \dfrac{2.641\ \text{g}}{44.01\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.06001\ \text{mol}$

$n(\ce{H2O}) = \dfrac{1.351\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.07497\ \text{mol}$, so $n(\ce{H}) = 2 \times 0.07497\ \text{mol} = 0.1499\ \text{mol}$

Ratio H : C = 0.1499 mol : 0.06001 mol = 2.498 : 1 ≈ 2.5 : 1 = 5 : 2, so the empirical formula is **$\ce{C2H5}$** (29.06 g/mol).

$\dfrac{58.1\ \text{g/mol}}{29.06\ \text{g/mol}} = 2.00$, so the molecular formula is **$\ce{C4H10}$**.

(b) **butane** and **2-methylpropane**.

</Answer>
