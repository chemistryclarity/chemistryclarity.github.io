## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) The reactant used up first, which limits the amount of product. (b) A reactant left over when the reaction stops. (c) The maximum amount of product, calculated from the limiting reactant.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Why yields are below 100%.</Q>
<Answer>Any two of: the reaction does not go to completion; side reactions form other products; product is lost during separation or purification.</Answer>

## B. Working in moles

<Q n={3}>3.0 mol H₂ + 2.0 mol O₂.</Q>
<Answer>3.0 mol H₂ × (1 mol O₂ / 2 mol H₂) = 1.5 mol O₂ needed, so (a) **H₂ is limiting**. (b) H₂O = 3.0 mol H₂ × (2 mol H₂O / 2 mol H₂) = **3.0 mol**. (c) O₂ left = 2.0 mol − 1.5 mol = **0.5 mol**.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>1.5 mol N₂ + 3.0 mol H₂.</Q>
<Answer>1.5 mol N₂ × (3 mol H₂ / 1 mol N₂) = 4.5 mol H₂ needed, but only 3.0 mol is available, so (a) **H₂ is limiting**. (b) NH₃ = 3.0 mol H₂ × (2 mol NH₃ / 3 mol H₂) = **2.0 mol**. (c) N₂ used = 3.0 mol H₂ × (1 mol N₂ / 3 mol H₂) = 1.0 mol, so N₂ left = 1.5 mol − 1.0 mol = **0.5 mol**.</Answer>

## C. Working in grams

<Q n={5}>5.40 g Al + 15.0 g Cl₂.</Q>
<Answer>

n(Al) = 5.40 g ÷ 26.98 g/mol = 0.2001 mol; n(Cl₂) = 15.0 g ÷ 70.90 g/mol = 0.2116 mol.

The Al would need 0.2001 mol Al × (3 mol Cl₂ / 2 mol Al) = 0.3002 mol Cl₂, but only 0.2116 mol is available, so (a) **Cl₂ is limiting**.

(b) n(AlCl₃) = 0.2116 mol Cl₂ × (2 mol AlCl₃ / 3 mol Cl₂) = 0.1410 mol; M(AlCl₃) = 133.33 g/mol; mass = 0.1410 mol × 133.33 g/mol = **18.8 g**.

(c) Al used = 0.1410 mol; Al left = 0.2001 mol − 0.1410 mol = 0.0591 mol; 0.0591 mol × 26.98 g/mol = **1.59 g**.

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Percent yield (15.2 g collected).</Q>
<Answer>Percent yield = 15.2 g ÷ 18.81 g × 100% = **80.8%** (using the unrounded theoretical yield, 18.81 g; the units of g cancel).</Answer>

<Q n={7}>10.0 g CH₄ + 32.0 g O₂.</Q>
<Answer>

n(CH₄) = 10.0 g ÷ 16.04 g/mol = 0.6234 mol; n(O₂) = 32.0 g ÷ 32.00 g/mol = 1.000 mol. The methane needs 0.6234 mol CH₄ × (2 mol O₂ / 1 mol CH₄) = 1.247 mol O₂, so (a) **O₂ is limiting**.

(b) n(CO₂) = 1.000 mol O₂ × (1 mol CO₂ / 2 mol O₂) = 0.500 mol; mass = 0.500 mol × 44.01 g/mol = **22.0 g**.

(c) CH₄ used = 0.500 mol × 16.04 g/mol = 8.02 g; CH₄ left = 10.0 g − 8.02 g = **1.98 g**.

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>A 108% yield.</Q>
<Answer>Most likely the product was still **wet** or contained **impurities**, which added mass. Mass cannot be created, so a pure, dry product can never exceed the theoretical yield.</Answer>
