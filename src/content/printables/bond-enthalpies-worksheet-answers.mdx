## A. Ideas

<Q n={1}>Bond enthalpy.</Q>
<Answer>The energy needed to **break one mole** of a bond in gaseous molecules to form gaseous atoms. Breaking a bond needs energy to overcome the attraction between the nuclei and the shared electrons (**endothermic**); forming a bond releases the same amount of energy (**exothermic**).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Bond order.</Q>
<Answer>Length, shortest first: **C≡C, C=C, C–C**. Strength, weakest first: **C–C, C=C, C≡C**. The second bond in C=C is a **π bond**, formed by side-on overlap, which is weaker than the σ bond, so C=C (614 kJ/mol) is less than 2 × 348 = 696 kJ/mol.</Answer>

## B. Estimating ΔH

<Q n={3}>H₂ + F₂.</Q>
<Answer>

Broken: $436\ \text{kJ/mol} + 155\ \text{kJ/mol} = 591\ \text{kJ/mol}$. Formed: $2 \times 567\ \text{kJ/mol} = 1134\ \text{kJ/mol}$.

$\Delta H \approx 591\ \text{kJ/mol} - 1134\ \text{kJ/mol} = \boldsymbol{-543\ \textbf{kJ/mol}}$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Chlorination of methane.</Q>
<Answer>

Broken: C–H + Cl–Cl $= 413\ \text{kJ/mol} + 242\ \text{kJ/mol} = 655\ \text{kJ/mol}$. Formed: C–Cl + H–Cl $= 328\ \text{kJ/mol} + 431\ \text{kJ/mol} = 759\ \text{kJ/mol}$.

$\Delta H \approx 655\ \text{kJ/mol} - 759\ \text{kJ/mol} = \boldsymbol{-104\ \textbf{kJ/mol}}$

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Hydrogenation of ethyne.</Q>
<Answer>

The two C–H bonds of ethyne are unchanged, so count only the bonds that change.

Broken: C≡C + 2 H–H $= 839\ \text{kJ/mol} + 2 \times 436\ \text{kJ/mol} = 1711\ \text{kJ/mol}$

Formed: C–C + 4 new C–H $= 348\ \text{kJ/mol} + 4 \times 413\ \text{kJ/mol} = 2000\ \text{kJ/mol}$

$\Delta H \approx 1711\ \text{kJ/mol} - 2000\ \text{kJ/mol} = \boldsymbol{-289\ \textbf{kJ/mol}}$

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Combustion of ethane.</Q>
<Answer>

Broken: 2 C₂H₆ contain 2 C–C and 12 C–H; plus 7 O=O:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(2 \times 348 + 12 \times 413 + 7 \times 498)\ \text{kJ} \\[4pt]
&= (696 + 4956 + 3486)\ \text{kJ} \\[4pt]
&= 9138\ \text{kJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

Formed: 4 CO₂ contain 8 C=O; 6 H₂O contain 12 O–H:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(8 \times 799 + 12 \times 463)\ \text{kJ} \\[4pt]
&= (6392 + 5556)\ \text{kJ} \\[4pt]
&= 11\,948\ \text{kJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

$\Delta H \approx 9138\ \text{kJ} - 11\,948\ \text{kJ} = \boldsymbol{-2810\ \textbf{kJ}}$ for the equation as written, which is $\boldsymbol{-1405\ \textbf{kJ}}$ **per mole of ethane**.

</Answer>

## C. Working backwards

<Q n={7}>H–I.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&-9\ \text{kJ/mol} = (436 + 151)\ \text{kJ/mol} - 2x \\[4pt]
&2x = 587\ \text{kJ/mol} + 9\ \text{kJ/mol} = 596\ \text{kJ/mol} \\[4pt]
&x = \textbf{298 kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Why the difference.</Q>
<Answer>(1) The measured value forms **liquid** water: condensing water vapour releases extra energy, which the gas-phase bond enthalpies leave out. (2) The bond enthalpies are **averages** over many compounds, not the exact values for the bonds in methane, carbon dioxide and water.</Answer>
