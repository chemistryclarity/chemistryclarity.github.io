## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Reaction types.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Addition:** two molecules form one, e.g. ethene + Br₂ → 1,2-dibromoethane. (b) **Substitution:** an atom or group is replaced, e.g. CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. (c) **Elimination:** a small molecule is removed and C=C forms, e.g. ethanol → ethene + H₂O. (d) **Oxidation:** gain of O or loss of H, e.g. ethanol → ethanal. (e) **Condensation:** two molecules join and release water, e.g. acid + alcohol → ester + water.</Answer>

## C. Tests and selectivity

<Q n={4}>Tests.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Shake with **bromine water**: hex-1-ene turns it from **orange-brown to colourless**; hexane leaves it orange. (b) Warm with **acidified potassium dichromate**: propan-1-ol (primary) turns it from **orange to green**; 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tertiary) leaves it **orange**.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Aldehyde or acid.</Q>
<Answer>To get **propanal**, use a limited amount of oxidizing agent and **distil** the aldehyde off as it forms (it boils lower than the alcohol), so it is not oxidized further. To get **propanoic acid**, use excess oxidizing agent and **heat under reflux**, so the aldehyde stays in the flask and is oxidized to the acid.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Propene + HBr.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **1-bromopropane** and **2-bromopropane**. (b) **2-bromopropane** is the major product (Markovnikov's rule): the H adds to the end carbon, which already has more hydrogens, and Br adds to the middle carbon.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Synthesis of ethyl ethanoate.</Q>
<Answer>1. Ethene + steam, acid catalyst → **ethanol** (**addition**, hydration). 2. Oxidize part of the ethanol with **excess acidified potassium dichromate, heated under reflux** → **ethanoic acid** (**oxidation**). 3. Warm ethanoic acid with the rest of the ethanol and a few drops of **concentrated sulfuric acid** → **ethyl ethanoate** + water (**condensation**, esterification).</Answer>
