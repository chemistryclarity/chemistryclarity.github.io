## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Collision theory.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Powder has a much **larger surface area**, so more particles are exposed to the acid and **collisions happen more often**. (b) A higher concentration means **more particles per litre**, so collisions are **more frequent**; the fraction of collisions with enough energy is unchanged, but there are more of them per second.</Answer>

## B. Rates and stoichiometry

<Q n={2}>Rates of formation.</Q>
<Answer>NO₂: 0.0060 M/s × (4 mol NO₂ / 2 mol N₂O₅) = **0.012 M/s**. O₂: 0.0060 M/s × (1 mol O₂ / 2 mol N₂O₅) = **0.0030 M/s**.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Average rate.</Q>
<Answer>$-\dfrac{\Delta[\ce{A}]}{\Delta t} = -\dfrac{0.560\ \text{M} - 0.800\ \text{M}}{120\ \text{s}} = \dfrac{0.240\ \text{M}}{120\ \text{s}} =$ **$2.00 \times 10^{-3}$ M/s**</Answer>

## C. Rate laws

<Q n={4}>rate = k[NO]²[H₂].</Q>
<Answer>(a) **second order** in NO, **first order** in H₂ (b) **third order** overall (c) k = rate ÷ ([NO]²[H₂]) = M s⁻¹ ÷ M³ = **M⁻² s⁻¹** (d) the rate is multiplied by 2² = **4**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Method of initial rates.</Q>
<Answer>

Experiments 1 → 2: [NO] doubles, [O₂] constant; rate ratio = $\dfrac{1.12 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{M/s}}{2.8 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M/s}} = 4$, so **second order in NO**.

Experiments 1 → 3: [O₂] doubles; rate ratio = $\dfrac{5.6 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M/s}}{2.8 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M/s}} = 2$, so **first order in O₂**.

Rate law: **rate = k[NO]²[O₂]**. From experiment 1:

$$
k = \frac{2.8 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{M s}^{-1}}{(0.020\ \text{M})^2(0.010\ \text{M})} = 0.70\ \text{M}^{-2}\ \text{s}^{-1}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Double [NO], halve [O₂].</Q>
<Answer>Rate is multiplied by 2² × ½ = **2**: the rate doubles.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Units of k.</Q>
<Answer>Zero order: **M s⁻¹**; first order: **s⁻¹**; second order: **M⁻¹ s⁻¹**</Answer>

## D. Half-life

<Q n={8}>k = 2.31 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{2.31 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}} =$ **300. s**

(b) 900. s ÷ 300. s = 3 half-lives, so $[\ce{A}] = 0.400\ \text{M} \times \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 =$ **0.0500 M**

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>k = 0.0150 min⁻¹, [A]₀ = 0.200 M.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $kt = 0.0150\ \text{min}^{-1} \times 60.0\ \text{min} = 0.900$ (no unit). $[\ce{A}] = 0.200\ \text{M} \times e^{-0.900} =$ **0.0813 M**

(b) From $\ln\dfrac{[\ce{A}]_0}{[\ce{A}]_t} = kt$:

$$
t = \frac{\ln(0.200\ \text{M} / 0.0500\ \text{M})}{0.0150\ \text{min}^{-1}} = \frac{1.386}{0.0150\ \text{min}^{-1}} = 92.4\ \text{min}
$$

(The concentration falls to a quarter, which is exactly 2 half-lives: 2 × 46.2 min = 92.4 min.)

</Answer>
