## A. Ideas

<Q n={1}>Phase changes.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **condensation**, exothermic (b) **sublimation**, endothermic (c) **evaporation** (vaporization), endothermic (d) **deposition**, exothermic (e) **freezing**, exothermic</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Constant temperature.</Q>
<Answer>The heat supplied is used to **overcome the intermolecular forces** (hydrogen bonds) as liquid turns into vapour, not to make the molecules move faster. The temperature only rises once all the water has boiled away, or if the pressure changes.</Answer>

## B. One-step calculations

<Q n={3}>Melting and freezing.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $n = \dfrac{100.\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} = 5.549\ \text{mol}$; $q = 5.549\ \text{mol} \times 6.01\ \text{kJ/mol} = \textbf{33.4 kJ}$

(b) $n = \dfrac{50.0\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} = 2.775\ \text{mol}$; $q = 2.775\ \text{mol} \times 6.01\ \text{kJ/mol} = 16.7\ \text{kJ}$, **released** (q = −16.7 kJ for the water).

</Answer>

## C. Multi-step calculations

<Q n={5}>Ice at −20 °C to water at 50 °C.</Q>
<Answer>

Warm ice: $q_1 = 10.0\ \text{g} \times 2.09\ \text{J/(g·°C)} \times 20.0\ \text{°C} = 418\ \text{J} = 0.418\ \text{kJ}$

Melt: $q_2 = \dfrac{10.0\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} \times 6.01\ \text{kJ/mol} = 3.335\ \text{kJ}$

Warm water: $q_3 = 10.0\ \text{g} \times 4.184\ \text{J/(g·°C)} \times 50.0\ \text{°C} = 2092\ \text{J} = 2.092\ \text{kJ}$

Total: $(0.418 + 3.335 + 2.092)\ \text{kJ} = \textbf{5.85 kJ}$

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Ice in a drink.</Q>
<Answer>

Melt: $\dfrac{200.\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} \times 6.01\ \text{kJ/mol} = 66.70\ \text{kJ}$

Warm: $200.\ \text{g} \times 4.184\ \text{J/(g·°C)} \times 20.0\ \text{°C} = 16\,736\ \text{J} = 16.74\ \text{kJ}$

Total: $66.70\ \text{kJ} + 16.74\ \text{kJ} = \textbf{83.4 kJ}$. Most of the cooling comes from melting.

</Answer>

## D. Vapour pressure and boiling

<Q n={7}>Everest.</Q>
<Answer>Water boils when its **vapour pressure equals the external pressure**. With the air pressure only about one third of an atmosphere, the vapour pressure of water reaches it at a much lower temperature (about 70 °C). The water cannot get hotter than its boiling point, so food cooks more slowly.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Steam versus water.</Q>
<Answer>

Water, 100 °C → 37 °C: $10.0\ \text{g} \times 4.184\ \text{J/(g·°C)} \times 63\ \text{°C} = 2636\ \text{J} = \textbf{2.64 kJ}$

Steam: condensing $\dfrac{10.0\ \text{g}}{18.02\ \text{g/mol}} \times 40.7\ \text{kJ/mol} = 22.59\ \text{kJ}$, then cooling 2.64 kJ: total **25.2 kJ**.

The steam releases almost **ten times** more heat, because condensing releases the large enthalpy of vaporization directly onto the skin.

</Answer>
