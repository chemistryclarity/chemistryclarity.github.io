## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) The spontaneous decay of **unstable nuclei**, which emit radiation (alpha, beta, positron or gamma). (b) The **time for half** of the radioactive nuclei in a sample (and its activity) to decay. (c) The decay of any one nucleus is **random**, but in a very large number of nuclei a constant **fraction** decays in each unit of time, so the sample as a whole follows the half-life.</Answer>

## B. Nuclei and nuclear equations

<Q n={2}>Protons and neutrons.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **92 p, 143 n** (b) **27 p, 33 n** (c) **53 p, 78 n** (d) **86 p, 136 n**. Neutrons = A − Z.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Identify X.</Q>
<Answer>(a) A = 0, Z = −1: a **beta particle**, $\ce{^{0}_{-1}e}$. (b) A = 4, Z = 2: an **alpha particle**, $\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$. (c) A = 0, Z = +1: a **positron**, $\ce{^{0}_{+1}e}$.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Uranium-238 series.</Q>
<Answer>$\ce{^{238}_{92}U -> ^{234}_{90}Th + ^{4}_{2}He}$; $\ce{^{234}_{90}Th -> ^{234}_{91}Pa + ^{0}_{-1}e}$; $\ce{^{234}_{91}Pa -> ^{234}_{92}U + ^{0}_{-1}e}$. Final nucleus: **uranium-234**, $\ce{^{234}_{92}U}$ (an isotope of the starting element).</Answer>

## C. Types of radiation

<Q n={6}>Comparing radiation.</Q>
<Answer>**Alpha:** a helium nucleus, charge +2; stopped by paper or skin. **Beta:** an electron, charge −1; stopped by a few mm of aluminium. **Gamma:** electromagnetic radiation, no charge; only reduced by thick lead or concrete. Inside the body, an alpha emitter deposits all its energy in a very short distance in living cells, and alpha particles ionize strongly, so they cause the most damage there.</Answer>

## D. Half-life

<Q n={7}>Technetium-99m.</Q>
<Answer>

$n = \dfrac{24.04\ \text{h}}{6.01\ \text{h}} = 4.00$ half-lives

$\text{activity} = 800\ \text{MBq} \times \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{4.00} = \dfrac{800\ \text{MBq}}{16} =$ **50.0 MBq**

</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Half-life from data, and radon.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) 1600 Bq → 800 Bq → 400 Bq is **2 half-lives**, so $t_{1/2} = \dfrac{30.0\ \text{min}}{2} =$ **15.0 min**

(b) $n = \dfrac{10.0\ \text{d}}{3.82\ \text{d}} = 2.618$; fraction remaining $= \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2.618} = 0.163$, so **16.3 %** remains.

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Radiocarbon age.</Q>
<Answer>

$\dfrac{N_0}{N} = \dfrac{0.230\ \text{Bq/g}}{0.0850\ \text{Bq/g}} = 2.706$ (the units cancel)

$n = \dfrac{\ln 2.706}{\ln 2} = \dfrac{0.9955}{0.6931} = 1.436$ half-lives

$t = 1.436 \times 5730\ \text{yr} =$ **8.23 × 10³ yr** (about 8230 years). Check: the activity has fallen to 37.0 %, between 50 % and 25 %, so the age lies between 1 and 2 half-lives. ✓

</Answer>
