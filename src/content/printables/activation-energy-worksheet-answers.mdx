## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>**Activation energy**: the minimum energy that colliding particles need in order to react. **Transition state**: the highest-energy, unstable arrangement of atoms on the energy profile, between reactants and products.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Energy profile.</Q>
<Answer>(a) ΔH is negative, so **exothermic**. (b) Ea (reverse) = 120 kJ/mol + 40 kJ/mol = **160 kJ/mol**. (c) The catalyst lowers both hills by 50 kJ/mol: Ea (reverse) = 70 kJ/mol + 40 kJ/mol = **110 kJ/mol**; ΔH is **unchanged, −40 kJ/mol**.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Maxwell–Boltzmann.</Q>
<Answer>Only molecules with energy ≥ Ea can react. At a higher temperature the distribution flattens and shifts to higher energy, so the **area beyond Ea grows much more than the average energy does**. A small temperature rise can therefore roughly double the number of molecules able to react (and they collide slightly more often too).</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Catalysts.</Q>
<Answer>A **homogeneous** catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants (e.g. H⁺ ions catalysing ester hydrolysis in solution). A **heterogeneous** catalyst is in a different phase, usually a solid surface (e.g. iron in the Haber process, platinum in catalytic converters). A catalyst **does not change K**.</Answer>

## B. The Arrhenius equation

<Q n={5}>Factor from 300. K to 310. K.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln\frac{k_2}{k_1} &= \frac{7.50 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/mol}}{8.314\ \text{J/(mol·K)}} \left(\frac{1}{300.\ \text{K}} - \frac{1}{310.\ \text{K}}\right) \\[4pt]
&= 0.970
\end{aligned}
$$

$\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = e^{0.970} =$ **2.64** (the units cancel inside the exponent)

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Ea from two rate constants.</Q>
<Answer>

$\ln\dfrac{6.0 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1}}{3.0 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}} = \ln 20. = 2.996$; $\dfrac{1}{500.\ \text{K}} - \dfrac{1}{550.\ \text{K}} = 1.818 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\text{a} &= \frac{8.314\ \text{J/(mol·K)} \times 2.996}{1.818 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{K}^{-1}} \\[4pt]
&= 1.37 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J/mol} = 137\ \text{kJ/mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

The rate constants have 2 significant figures, so report **Ea = 1.4 × 10² kJ/mol** (137 kJ/mol before rounding).

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>k at 330. K.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln\frac{k_2}{k_1} &= \frac{7.50 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/mol}}{8.314\ \text{J/(mol·K)}} \left(\frac{1}{300.\ \text{K}} - \frac{1}{330.\ \text{K}}\right) \\[4pt]
&= 2.734
\end{aligned}
$$

$k_2 = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{s}^{-1} \times e^{2.734} =$ **$3.1 \times 10^{-3}$ s⁻¹** (3.08 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ before rounding to 2 significant figures)

</Answer>

## C. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Effect of the catalyst.</Q>
<Answer>

With A unchanged, $\dfrac{k_\text{cat}}{k} = e^{(E_\text{a} - E_\text{a,cat})/RT}$:

$$
\frac{(7.50 - 5.00) \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/mol}}{8.314\ \text{J/(mol·K)} \times 298\ \text{K}} = 10.09
$$

$\dfrac{k_\text{cat}}{k} = e^{10.09} =$ **$2.4 \times 10^{4}$**: about 24 000 times faster.

</Answer>

<Q n={9}>Arrhenius plot.</Q>
<Answer>Slope $= -\dfrac{E_\text{a}}{R}$, so $E_\text{a} = 6.65 \times 10^{3}\ \text{K} \times 8.314\ \text{J/(mol·K)} = 5.53 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/mol} =$ **55.3 kJ/mol** (the units of K cancel).</Answer>
