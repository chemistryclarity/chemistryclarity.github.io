## A. Writing K

<Q n={1}>Kp expressions.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $K_\text{p} = \dfrac{(P_{\ce{SO3}})^2}{(P_{\ce{SO2}})^2\,P_{\ce{O2}}}$ (b) $K_\text{p} = \dfrac{(P_{\ce{NO2}})^2}{(P_{\ce{NO}})^2\,P_{\ce{O2}}}$

(c) $K_\text{p} = P_{\ce{H2O}}$ (the vapour pressure; liquid left out) (d) $K_\text{p} = P_{\ce{NH3}}\,P_{\ce{HCl}}$ (solid left out)

</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Δn.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **−1**: Kp smaller than Kc (b) **−1**: smaller (c) **+1**: Kp larger than Kc (d) **+2**: larger. At 25 °C, RT = 24.47, which is greater than 1, so a positive Δn makes Kp larger and a negative Δn makes it smaller.</Answer>

## B. Calculating Kp and Kc

<Q n={3}>Kp from pressures.</Q>
<Answer>$K_\text{p} = \dfrac{(1.2)^2}{(0.20)^2 \times 0.10} = \dfrac{1.44}{0.0040} = \textbf{360}$</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Kc to Kp.</Q>
<Answer>

$RT = 0.08206\ \text{L atm/(mol K)} \times 1000\ \text{K} = 82.06\ \text{L atm/mol}$; Δn = −1

$K_\text{p} = 280 \times (82.06)^{-1} = \textbf{3.41}$

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Kp to Kc.</Q>
<Answer>

$RT = 0.08206\ \text{L atm/(mol K)} \times 298.15\ \text{K} = 24.47\ \text{L atm/mol}$; Δn = +1

$K_\text{c} = \dfrac{K_\text{p}}{(RT)^{\Delta n}} = \dfrac{0.15}{24.47} = \boldsymbol{6.1 \times 10^{-3}}$

</Answer>

## C. Q and ICE tables

<Q n={6}>Q and direction.</Q>
<Answer>$Q = \dfrac{(0.50)^2}{0.10 \times 0.10} = \textbf{25}$. Q (25) is less than K (50), so the reaction goes **forward**, making more HI.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>ICE table.</Q>
<Answer>

ICE (atm): N₂O₄: $1.00 - x$; NO₂: $2x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{(2x)^2}{1.00 - x} = 0.15 \\[4pt]
&4x^2 + 0.15x - 0.15 = 0 \\[4pt]
&x = \frac{-0.15 + \sqrt{0.15^2 + 4 \times 4 \times 0.15}}{2 \times 4} = 0.176
\end{aligned}
$$

$P_{\ce{NO2}} = 2 \times 0.176 = \textbf{0.352 atm}$; $P_{\ce{N2O4}} = 1.00 - 0.176 = \textbf{0.824 atm}$. Check: $(0.352)^2 \div 0.824 = 0.150$ ✓

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Limestone in a sealed container.</Q>
<Answer>Kp = P(CO₂), which is fixed at a given temperature. (a) Adding more solid CaCO₃ does **not change** P(CO₂): solids are not in the K expression. (b) Removing CO₂ makes Q less than K, so more CaCO₃ decomposes until **P(CO₂) returns** to the value of Kp.</Answer>
