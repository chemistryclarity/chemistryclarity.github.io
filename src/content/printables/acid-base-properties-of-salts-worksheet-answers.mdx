## A. Predicting

<Q n={1}>Acidic, basic or neutral.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **neutral** (no ion hydrolyses) (b) **basic** ($\ce{CO3^2-}$) (c) **acidic** ($\ce{NH4+}$) (d) **basic** ($\ce{CH3COO-}$) (e) **neutral** (f) **acidic** ($\ce{NH4+}$)</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Hydrolysis equations.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{F-(aq) + H2O(l) <=> HF(aq) + OH-(aq)}$

(b) $\ce{NH4+(aq) + H2O(l) <=> NH3(aq) + H3O+(aq)}$

</Answer>

## B. Ka and Kb

<Q n={3}>Ka and Kb.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $K_\text{b}(\ce{F-}) = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{6.8 \times 10^{-4}} = \boldsymbol{1.5 \times 10^{-11}}$

(b) $K_\text{a}(\ce{NH4+}) = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{1.8 \times 10^{-5}} = \boldsymbol{5.6 \times 10^{-10}}$

(c) $K_\text{b}(\ce{CN-}) = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{4.9 \times 10^{-10}} = \boldsymbol{2.0 \times 10^{-5}}$

**CN⁻** is the stronger base (larger Kb), because HCN is a much weaker acid than HF.

</Answer>

## C. pH of salt solutions

<Q n={4}>0.10 mol/L NaCN.</Q>
<Answer>

$[\ce{OH-}] \approx \sqrt{2.0 \times 10^{-5} \times 0.10} = 1.41 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol/L}$ (1.4 % of 0.10 mol/L: approximation OK)

pOH $= -\log(1.41 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.85$; pH $= 14.00 - 2.85 = \textbf{11.15}$

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>0.050 mol/L NH₄NO₃.</Q>
<Answer>

$[\ce{H3O+}] \approx \sqrt{5.6 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.050} = 5.29 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{mol/L}$

pH $= -\log(5.29 \times 10^{-6}) = \textbf{5.28}$

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>0.25 mol/L CH₃COOK.</Q>
<Answer>

$K_\text{b}(\ce{CH3COO-}) = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$; $[\ce{OH-}] \approx \sqrt{5.6 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.25} = 1.18 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{mol/L}$

pOH $= 4.93$; pH $= 14.00 - 4.93 = \textbf{9.07}$

</Answer>

## D. Titrations

<Q n={7}>Equivalence point.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Same concentration, 1 : 1 reaction: **25.00 mL** of NaOH.

(b) Moles of ethanoate $= 0.100\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.02500\ \text{L} = 2.50 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$, in $0.05000\ \text{L}$: $c = \textbf{0.0500 mol/L}$

(c) $[\ce{OH-}] \approx \sqrt{5.6 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.0500} = 5.29 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{mol/L}$; pOH $= 5.28$; pH $= \textbf{8.72}$. **Phenolphthalein** (changes colour at about pH 8.2–10) is suitable.

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Aluminium chloride.</Q>
<Answer>In water, $\ce{Al^3+}$ is surrounded by six water molecules, $\ce{[Al(H2O)6]^3+}$. The small, highly charged ion pulls electron density from the O–H bonds of these water molecules, so one of them can release a proton: $\ce{[Al(H2O)6]^3+ + H2O <=> [Al(H2O)5(OH)]^2+ + H3O+}$. The $\ce{H3O+}$ formed makes the solution **acidic**.</Answer>
