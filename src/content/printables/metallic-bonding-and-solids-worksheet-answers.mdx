## A. Metallic bonding

<Q n={1}>Metallic bonding.</Q>
<Answer>(a) The attraction between **positive metal ions** in a regular lattice and a **sea of delocalized electrons**. (b) The delocalized electrons are **free to move**, so they carry a current. The layers of ions can **slide** over each other while the electron sea keeps holding them together, so the metal changes shape without breaking.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Magnesium and sodium.</Q>
<Answer>Magnesium gives **two** electrons per atom to the sea (forming $\ce{Mg^2+}$), sodium only one ($\ce{Na+}$). Magnesium ions are also **smaller** and more highly charged, so the attraction between the ions and the electron sea is **stronger**, and more energy is needed to melt it.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Alloys.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) A mixture of a metal with other elements (usually metals).

(b) Tin atoms are a different size from copper atoms, so they **disrupt the layers**, which can no longer slide easily: the alloy is harder.

(c) $m_\text{Cu} = \dfrac{88.0}{100} \times 40.0\ \text{g} = \textbf{35.2 g}$ and $m_\text{Sn} = 40.0\ \text{g} - 35.2\ \text{g} = \textbf{4.8 g}$

</Answer>

## B. Types of solid

<Q n={4}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>P: **metallic** (conducts as a solid: mobile electrons). Q: **molecular** (low melting point, no conduction). R: **ionic** (conducts only when the ions are free to move). S: **covalent network** (strong covalent bonds throughout, no mobile charges).</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Explanations.</Q>
<Answer>(a) In solid KCl the ions are fixed in the lattice. In solution they are **free to move** and carry charge. (b) Iodine is a **molecular** solid. Melting only overcomes the weak **forces between** $\ce{I2}$ molecules; the I–I covalent bonds are not broken.</Answer>

## C. Diamond and graphite

<Q n={6}>Diamond and graphite.</Q>
<Answer>(a) In diamond every carbon is covalently bonded to **four** others in a rigid 3D network; many strong bonds must be broken to scratch or break it. (b) In graphite each carbon bonds to **three** others in flat layers; the fourth outer electron is **delocalized** along the layer and can move, carrying a current. (c) The layers are held together only by **weak forces**, so they slide off onto the paper.</Answer>

## D. Comparing structures

<Q n={7}>Bend or shatter.</Q>
<Answer>In the metal, layers of positive ions slide over each other but stay surrounded by the electron sea, so the bonding is not broken: it **bends**. In salt, a slight shift brings ions of the **same charge** next to each other; they repel and the crystal **shatters**.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>SiO₂ and CO₂.</Q>
<Answer>$\ce{CO2}$ is **molecular**: separate small molecules held together only by weak forces, which are overcome far below room temperature. $\ce{SiO2}$ is a **covalent network**: each Si is bonded to four O atoms and each O to two Si atoms in a giant 3D structure, so melting requires breaking many strong **covalent bonds**. The formula $\ce{SiO2}$ gives only the ratio of atoms, not a molecule.</Answer>
