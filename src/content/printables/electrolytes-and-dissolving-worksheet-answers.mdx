## A. Ideas

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Solute**: the substance dissolved. **Solvent**: the substance it dissolves in. **Electrolyte**: a substance that forms ions in solution, so the solution conducts. (b) Salt separates into **mobile ions** ($\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$) that carry charge. Sugar dissolves as **whole molecules** with no charge, so nothing carries the current.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Classify.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **strong** (b) **non-electrolyte** (c) **strong** (d) **weak** (e) **strong** (f) **non-electrolyte**</Answer>

## B. Dissociation and ion concentrations

<Q n={3}>Dissociation equations.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{K2SO4(s) -> 2K+(aq) + SO4^2-(aq)}$

(b) $\ce{Ca(NO3)2(s) -> Ca^2+(aq) + 2NO3-(aq)}$

(c) $\ce{(NH4)3PO4(s) -> 3NH4+(aq) + PO4^3-(aq)}$

(d) $\ce{FeCl3(s) -> Fe^3+(aq) + 3Cl-(aq)}$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Ion concentrations.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $[\ce{K+}] = 2 \times 0.200\ \text{mol/L} = \textbf{0.400 mol/L}$; $[\ce{SO4^2-}] = \textbf{0.200 mol/L}$ (b) $[\ce{Fe^3+}] = \textbf{0.150 mol/L}$; $[\ce{Cl-}] = 3 \times 0.150\ \text{mol/L} = \textbf{0.450 mol/L}$</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Best conductor.</Q>
<Answer>**CaCl₂**: it gives 3 ions per formula unit, 0.30 mol/L of ions in total. NaCl gives 0.20 mol/L of ions; ethanoic acid is only slightly ionized; glucose gives no ions.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Mixing.</Q>
<Answer>

Total volume $= 0.0500\ \text{L} + 0.0500\ \text{L} = 0.1000\ \text{L}$

$\ce{Ca^2+}$: $0.100\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.0500\ \text{L} = 5.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$, so $[\ce{Ca^2+}] = \dfrac{5.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.1000\ \text{L}} = \textbf{0.0500 mol/L}$

$\ce{K+}$: $0.200\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.0500\ \text{L} = 1.00 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}$, so $[\ce{K+}] = \textbf{0.100 mol/L}$

$\ce{Cl-}$: $2 \times 5.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} + 1.00 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol} = 2.00 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}$, so $[\ce{Cl-}] = \textbf{0.200 mol/L}$

</Answer>

## C. Energy changes

<Q n={7}>Hot or cold.</Q>
<Answer>Dissolving an ionic solid needs energy to **break up the lattice** (endothermic) and releases energy when the ions are **hydrated** (exothermic). If hydration releases **more** energy than the lattice needs, the overall change is exothermic and the water warms (e.g. CaCl₂). If it releases **less**, the overall change is endothermic and the energy comes from the water, which cools (e.g. NH₄NO₃).</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Hand warmer.</Q>
<Answer>

$n = \dfrac{5.00\ \text{g}}{110.98\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.04505\ \text{mol}$

Heat released: $0.04505\ \text{mol} \times 82.8\ \text{kJ/mol} = 3.730\ \text{kJ} = 3730\ \text{J}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta T = \frac{q}{mc} = \frac{3730\ \text{J}}{105\ \text{g} \times 4.184\ \text{J/(g·°C)}} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{8.49 °C rise}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>
