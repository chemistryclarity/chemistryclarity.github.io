## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Empirical vs molecular formula.</Q>
<Answer>The **empirical** formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms; the **molecular** formula gives the actual number of each kind of atom in one molecule.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Empirical formulas.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{CH}$ (b) $\ce{CH2}$ (c) $\ce{HO}$ (d) $\ce{C4H9}$ (e) $\ce{NO2}$</Answer>

<Q n={3}>What is wrong with rounding 1 : 1.50?</Q>
<Answer>1.50 is not close to a whole number, so it must not be rounded. Multiply both values by 2: **2 : 3**, giving $\ce{A2B3}$.</Answer>

## B. Empirical formulas from percent composition

<Q n={4}>85.63% C, 14.37% H.</Q>
<Answer>C: 85.63 ÷ 12.01 = 7.130 mol; H: 14.37 ÷ 1.008 = 14.26 mol. Divide by 7.130: C = 1.00, H = 2.00. Empirical formula **$\ce{CH2}$**.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>40.04% Ca, 12.00% C, 47.96% O.</Q>
<Answer>Ca: 40.04 ÷ 40.08 = 0.9990; C: 12.00 ÷ 12.01 = 0.9992; O: 47.96 ÷ 16.00 = 2.998. Divide by 0.9990: 1 : 1 : 3. Empirical formula **$\ce{CaCO3}$**.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>72.36% Fe, 27.64% O.</Q>
<Answer>Fe: 72.36 ÷ 55.85 = 1.296; O: 27.64 ÷ 16.00 = 1.728. Divide by 1.296: Fe = 1.000, O = 1.333. A ratio ending in .33 means multiply by 3: 3 : 4. Empirical formula **$\ce{Fe3O4}$**.</Answer>

## C. Molecular formulas

<Q n={7}>CH₂O, molar mass 60.05 g/mol.</Q>
<Answer>Empirical formula mass = 12.01 + 2(1.008) + 16.00 = 30.03 g/mol. $n = 60.05 \div 30.03 = 2.00$. Molecular formula **$\ce{C2H4O2}$**.</Answer>

<Q n={8}>CH, molar mass 26.04 g/mol.</Q>
<Answer>Empirical formula mass = 13.02 g/mol. $n = 26.04 \div 13.02 = 2.00$. Molecular formula **$\ce{C2H2}$**.</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={9}>30.43% N, molar mass 92.02 g/mol.</Q>
<Answer>

O = 100 − 30.43 = 69.57%. N: 30.43 ÷ 14.01 = 2.172 mol; O: 69.57 ÷ 16.00 = 4.348 mol. Ratio N : O = 1 : 2.00, so the empirical formula is **$\ce{NO2}$** (46.01 g/mol).

$n = 92.02 \div 46.01 = 2.00$, so the molecular formula is **$\ce{N2O4}$**.

</Answer>
