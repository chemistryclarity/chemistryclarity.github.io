## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Concepts.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Using electricity to drive a **non-spontaneous** redox reaction. (b) An electrolytic cell **uses** electrical energy, a galvanic cell **produces** it; in electrolysis the anode is **positive**, in a galvanic cell it is negative. (c) The **ions must be free to move** to the electrodes; in a solid ionic compound they are fixed in the lattice.</Answer>

## B. Predicting products

<Q n={2}>Molten compounds.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Cathode **potassium**, anode **bromine** (b) cathode **magnesium**, anode **chlorine** (c) cathode **aluminium**, anode **oxygen**</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Aqueous solutions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Cathode **copper**, anode **chlorine** (b) cathode **hydrogen**, anode **oxygen** (c) cathode **silver**, anode **oxygen** (d) cathode **hydrogen**, anode **iodine**</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Half-equations.</Q>
<Answer>(b) Cathode: $\ce{2H2O + 2e- -> H2 + 2OH-}$; anode: $\ce{2H2O -> O2 + 4H+ + 4e-}$. (c) Cathode: $\ce{Ag+ + e- -> Ag}$; anode: $\ce{2H2O -> O2 + 4H+ + 4e-}$.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Copper refining.</Q>
<Answer>The **impure copper anode** is oxidized and dissolves: $\ce{Cu -> Cu^2+ + 2e-}$. At the **pure copper cathode**, $\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e- -> Cu}$ deposits pure copper. Less reactive impurities (silver, gold) do not dissolve and fall to the bottom as **anode sludge**; more reactive ones dissolve but stay in solution.</Answer>

## C. Faraday's law

<Q n={6}>Silver plating.</Q>
<Answer>

$Q = (0.500\ \text{C/s})(30.0\ \text{min} \times 60\ \text{s/min}) = 900.\ \text{C}$; $n(e^-) = \dfrac{900.\ \text{C}}{96\,485\ \text{C/mol}} = 9.328 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

1 mol e⁻ per mol Ag: $m = 9.328 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \times 107.87\ \text{g/mol} =$ **1.01 g**

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Copper deposition time.</Q>
<Answer>

$n(\ce{Cu}) = \dfrac{2.00\ \text{g}}{63.55\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.03147\ \text{mol}$; $n(e^-) = 2 \times 0.03147\ \text{mol} = 0.06294\ \text{mol}$

$Q = 0.06294\ \text{mol} \times 96\,485\ \text{C/mol} = 6073\ \text{C}$; $t = \dfrac{6073\ \text{C}}{1.50\ \text{C/s}} = 4049\ \text{s} =$ **67.5 min**

</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Electrolysis of water.</Q>
<Answer>

$Q = (0.500\ \text{C/s})(7200\ \text{s}) = 3600\ \text{C}$; $n(e^-) = \dfrac{3600\ \text{C}}{96\,485\ \text{C/mol}} = 0.03731\ \text{mol}$

$\ce{H2}$ (2 e⁻ each): $n = 0.01866\ \text{mol}$; $V = \dfrac{(0.01866\ \text{mol})(0.08206\ \text{L atm mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1})(298.15\ \text{K})}{1.00\ \text{atm}} =$ **0.456 L**

$\ce{O2}$ (4 e⁻ each): $n = 9.328 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$; $V =$ **0.228 L** (half the volume of hydrogen, as expected from $\ce{2H2O -> 2H2 + O2}$)

</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Energy for aluminium.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $n(\ce{Al}) = \dfrac{1.00 \times 10^{3}\ \text{g}}{26.98\ \text{g/mol}} = 37.06\ \text{mol}$; $n(e^-) = 3 \times 37.06\ \text{mol} = 111.2\ \text{mol}$

$Q = 111.2\ \text{mol} \times 96\,485\ \text{C/mol} =$ **1.07 × 10⁷ C**

(b) $E = QV = (1.073 \times 10^{7}\ \text{C})(4.5\ \text{V}) =$ **4.8 × 10⁷ J**; $\dfrac{4.83 \times 10^{7}\ \text{J}}{3.6 \times 10^{6}\ \text{J/kWh}} =$ **13 kWh** per kilogram

(c) Aluminium production needs **huge amounts of electricity**, which is the main cost, so smelters are built where electricity is cheap and plentiful (and, with hydroelectric power, low in carbon emissions).

</Answer>
