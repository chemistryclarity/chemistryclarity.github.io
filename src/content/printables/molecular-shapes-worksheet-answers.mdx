## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>VSEPR.</Q>
<Answer>Electron domains (bonds and lone pairs) around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves **as far apart as possible**, which sets the shape of the molecule.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Domain geometry vs shape.</Q>
<Answer>The electron-domain geometry describes **all** domains, including lone pairs; the molecular shape describes only the positions of the **atoms**. Water has 4 domains (tetrahedral electron-domain geometry), but 2 are lone pairs, so its molecular shape is **bent**.</Answer>

## B. Predicting shapes

<Q n={3}>Shapes.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{BCl3}$: 3 domains, 0 lone pairs, **trigonal planar**, 120° (b) $\ce{H2S}$: 4 domains, 2 lone pairs, **bent**, less than 109.5° (c) $\ce{PCl3}$: 4 domains, 1 lone pair, **trigonal pyramidal**, less than 109.5° (d) $\ce{CS2}$: 2 domains (two C=S), 0 lone pairs, **linear**, 180° (e) $\ce{SiH4}$: 4 domains, 0 lone pairs, **tetrahedral**, 109.5°</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Formaldehyde.</Q>
<Answer>Carbon has 3 domains (two C–H bonds and one C=O double bond, counted once) and no lone pairs: **trigonal planar**, H–C–H about **120°**.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Ammonium.</Q>
<Answer>N has 4 bonding domains and no lone pairs: **tetrahedral**, 109.5°.</Answer>

## C. Bond angles

<Q n={6}>Trend.</Q>
<Answer>All three have 4 electron domains. Lone pairs repel **more strongly** than bonding pairs, so they push the bonds closer together. $\ce{CH4}$ has 0 lone pairs, $\ce{NH3}$ has 1 and $\ce{H2O}$ has 2, so the angle decreases as the number of lone pairs increases.</Answer>

## D. Polarity

<Q n={7}>Polar or nonpolar.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{CO2}$: **nonpolar**; linear, so the two C=O polarities cancel (b) $\ce{H2O}$: **polar**; bent, so the O–H polarities don't cancel (c) $\ce{CCl4}$: **nonpolar**; tetrahedral with four identical bonds, so they cancel (d) $\ce{NH3}$: **polar**; trigonal pyramidal, so the N–H polarities don't cancel</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>XeF₄.</Q>
<Answer>Six domains point to the corners of an octahedron. The two lone pairs go **opposite each other** (180° apart) to minimise lone pair–lone pair repulsion, leaving the four F atoms in a square: **square planar**, F–Xe–F 90°.</Answer>

<Q n={9}>SF₄.</Q>
<Answer>Five domains with one lone pair, which sits in an equatorial position: **seesaw** shape.</Answer>
