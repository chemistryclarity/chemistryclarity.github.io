## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Particles and pressure.</Q>
<Answer>Pressure comes from gas particles colliding with the container walls. In a smaller volume the same particles hit the walls **more often**, so the pressure is higher.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Why kelvin?</Q>
<Answer>Volume and pressure are proportional to the **absolute** temperature, which starts at absolute zero (0 K). The Celsius scale starts at an arbitrary point, so ratios of Celsius temperatures are meaningless (and 0 °C would mean dividing by zero).</Answer>

## B. Simple gas laws

<Q n={3}>Boyle.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
V_2 = \frac{P_1V_1}{P_2} = \frac{(100\ \text{kPa})(5.0\ \text{L})}{250\ \text{kPa}} = \textbf{2.0 L}
$$

The units of kPa cancel, leaving L.

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Charles.</Q>
<Answer>

$T_1 = 20\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 293.15\ \text{K}$; $T_2 = -30\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 243.15\ \text{K}$

$$
V_2 = V_1 \times \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 250\ \text{mL} \times \frac{243.15\ \text{K}}{293.15\ \text{K}} = \textbf{207 mL}
$$

The units of K cancel, leaving mL.

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Gay-Lussac.</Q>
<Answer>

$T_1 = 15\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 288.15\ \text{K}$; $T_2 = 45\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 318.15\ \text{K}$

$$
P_2 = P_1 \times \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 220\ \text{kPa} \times \frac{318.15\ \text{K}}{288.15\ \text{K}} = \textbf{243 kPa}
$$

The units of K cancel, leaving kPa.

</Answer>

## C. Combined gas law

<Q n={6}>Combined.</Q>
<Answer>

$T_1 = 25\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 298.15\ \text{K}$; $T_2 = 100\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 373.15\ \text{K}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_2 &= \frac{P_1V_1T_2}{P_2T_1} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{(1.00\ \text{atm})(4.00\ \text{L})(373.15\ \text{K})}{(2.50\ \text{atm})(298.15\ \text{K})} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{2.00 L}
\end{aligned}
$$

The units of atm and K cancel, leaving L.

</Answer>

## D. Ideal gas law

<Q n={7}>Pressure.</Q>
<Answer>

Volume is in L and the answer is wanted in atm, so use $R = 0.08206\ \text{L·atm/(mol·K)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= \frac{nRT}{V} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{(0.250\ \text{mol}) \left(0.08206\ \dfrac{\text{L·atm}}{\text{mol·K}}\right) (300\ \text{K})}{5.00\ \text{L}} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{1.23 atm}
\end{aligned}
$$

The units of mol, K and L cancel, leaving atm.

</Answer>

<Q n={8}>Carbon dioxide.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $T = 27\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 300.15\ \text{K}$

Pressure is in atm, so use $R = 0.08206\ \text{L·atm/(mol·K)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{PV}{RT} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{(1.50\ \text{atm})(2.00\ \text{L})}{\left(0.08206\ \dfrac{\text{L·atm}}{\text{mol·K}}\right) (300.15\ \text{K})} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{0.122 mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

The units of atm, L and K cancel, leaving mol.

(b) $M(\ce{CO2}) = 12.01\ \text{g/mol} + 2(16.00\ \text{g/mol}) = 44.01\ \text{g/mol}$

$$
m = n \times M = 0.12181\ \text{mol} \times 44.01\ \text{g/mol} = \textbf{5.36 g}
$$

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Molar mass.</Q>
<Answer>

$T = 0\ °\text{C} + 273.15 = 273.15\ \text{K}$

Pressure is in atm, so use $R = 0.08206\ \text{L·atm/(mol·K)}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{PV}{RT} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{(1.00\ \text{atm})(0.560\ \text{L})}{\left(0.08206\ \dfrac{\text{L·atm}}{\text{mol·K}}\right) (273.15\ \text{K})} \\[4pt]
&= 0.02498\ \text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
M = \frac{m}{n} = \frac{1.00\ \text{g}}{0.02498\ \text{mol}} = \textbf{40.0 g/mol}
$$

The gas is **argon** (39.95 g/mol).

</Answer>
