## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Galvanic cell.</Q>
<Answer>**Oxidation** happens at the **anode** (negative) and **reduction** at the **cathode** (positive). Electrons flow through the wire **from anode to cathode**. The **salt bridge** lets ions move between the half-cells so that the solutions stay electrically neutral and the current can continue.</Answer>

## B. Cell potentials

<Q n={2}>Zinc–silver cell.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Ag⁺/Ag has the more positive E°, so silver is the **cathode**; zinc is the **anode**. (b) E°cell = +0.80 V − (−0.76 V) = **+1.56 V** (c) $\ce{Zn + 2Ag+ -> Zn^2+ + 2Ag}$ (d) $\ce{Zn(s) | Zn^2+(aq) || Ag+(aq) | Ag(s)}$</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Nickel–silver cell.</Q>
<Answer>E°cell = +0.80 V − (−0.25 V) = **+1.05 V**; **nickel** is the anode.</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Magnesium–copper cell.</Q>
<Answer>E°cell = +0.34 V − (−2.37 V) = **+2.71 V** (magnesium is the anode).</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Spontaneous?</Q>
<Answer>(a) Zn²⁺ would be reduced (cathode) and Cu oxidized (anode): E°cell = −0.76 V − (+0.34 V) = **−1.10 V**, so **not spontaneous**. (b) H⁺ reduced, Zn oxidized: E°cell = 0.00 V − (−0.76 V) = **+0.76 V**, so **spontaneous** (zinc dissolves in acid).</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Strongest agents.</Q>
<Answer>Strongest oxidizing agent: **F₂** (most positive E°, +2.87 V). Strongest reducing agent: **Mg** (most negative E°, −2.37 V, so Mg is most easily oxidized).</Answer>

## C. Free energy

<Q n={7}>ΔG° for the Daniell cell.</Q>
<Answer>

E°cell = +0.34 V − (−0.76 V) = +1.10 V; n = 2 mol of electrons.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta G° &= -(2\ \text{mol})\left(96\,485\ \tfrac{\text{C}}{\text{mol}}\right)(1.10\ \text{V}) \\[4pt]
&= -2.12 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} = -212\ \text{kJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

(mol cancels, and C × V = J.)

</Answer>

## D. Electrolysis

<Q n={8}>Silver plating.</Q>
<Answer>

$t = 45.0\ \text{min} \times 60\ \text{s/min} = 2700\ \text{s}$; $Q = 1.50\ \text{C/s} \times 2700\ \text{s} = 4050\ \text{C}$

$n(e^-) = \dfrac{4050\ \text{C}}{96\,485\ \text{C/mol}} = 0.04198\ \text{mol}$; 1 mol Ag per mol e⁻, so $n(\ce{Ag}) = 0.04198\ \text{mol}$

$m = 0.04198\ \text{mol} \times 107.87\ \text{g/mol} =$ **4.53 g**

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Time to deposit 5.00 g Cu.</Q>
<Answer>

$n(\ce{Cu}) = \dfrac{5.00\ \text{g}}{63.55\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.07868\ \text{mol}$; $n(e^-) = 2 \times 0.07868\ \text{mol} = 0.1574\ \text{mol}$

$Q = 0.1574\ \text{mol} \times 96\,485\ \text{C/mol} = 1.518 \times 10^{4}\ \text{C}$

$t = \dfrac{Q}{I} = \dfrac{1.518 \times 10^{4}\ \text{C}}{3.00\ \text{C/s}} = 5.06 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s}$, and $5.06 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{min}}{60\ \text{s}} =$ **84.3 min**

</Answer>
