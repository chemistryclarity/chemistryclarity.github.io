## A. Hazards

<Q n={1}>Pictograms.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Flammable**: ethanol (b) **Oxidizer**: potassium nitrate or concentrated hydrogen peroxide (c) **Corrosive**: concentrated acids or sodium hydroxide (d) **Acutely toxic**: mercury compounds (e) **Toxic to aquatic life**: copper(II) sulfate. Other correct examples are acceptable.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Signal words and the SDS.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Danger** (Warning is less severe). (b) A **safety data sheet** gives a chemical's hazards, safe handling, storage, first aid and disposal. Read it **before** using the chemical, as part of the risk assessment.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Five unsafe actions.</Q>
<Answer>

1. Goggles pushed up: keep **goggles over the eyes** whenever chemicals are in use.
2. Water poured into concentrated acid: add the **acid slowly to water**.
3. Ethanol heated over a flame: it is flammable, so use a **water bath** or hot plate.
4. Test tube pointed at her partner: point it **away from everyone**.
5. Drinking in the lab: **no eating or drinking**. (Also: copper(II) sulfate is toxic to aquatic life, so it goes in the **waste container**, not down the sink.)

</Answer>

## B. Choosing equipment

<Q n={4}>Equipment.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **25 mL volumetric pipette** (b) **measuring cylinder** (or beaker) (c) **250 mL volumetric flask** (d) **burette** (e) **analytical balance**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Percent uncertainty.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\dfrac{0.01\ \text{g}}{0.50\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = \textbf{2\%}$ and $\dfrac{0.0001\ \text{g}}{0.5000\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = \textbf{0.02\%}$

(b) $\dfrac{0.5\ \text{mL}}{10.0\ \text{mL}} \times 100\% = \textbf{5\%}$ and $\dfrac{0.02\ \text{mL}}{10.00\ \text{mL}} \times 100\% = \textbf{0.2\%}$

The analytical balance and the pipette are about 100 and 25 times more precise. Small quantities need precise equipment, and a large cylinder is a poor choice for a small volume.

</Answer>

## C. Burette readings

<Q n={6}>Titres.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Rough: 24.10 mL − 0.00 mL = **24.10 mL**. Run 1: 24.70 mL − 1.20 mL = **23.50 mL**. Run 2: 23.85 mL − 0.40 mL = **23.45 mL**. Run 3: 23.55 mL − 0.05 mL = **23.50 mL**.

(b) Mean of runs 1–3:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bar{V} = \frac{(23.50 + 23.45 + 23.50)\ \text{mL}}{3} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{70.45\ \text{mL}}{3} = 23.483\ \text{mL}
\end{aligned}
$$

Deviations: +0.017 mL, −0.033 mL, +0.017 mL.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&s = \sqrt{\frac{1.67 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mL}^2}{3 - 1}} \\[4pt]
&= 0.029\ \text{mL}
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: **23.48 ± 0.03 mL (n = 3)**. The rough titre is left out: it only shows roughly where the end point is.

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>The meniscus.</Q>
<Answer>Water forms a curved surface that dips in the middle; the calibration marks are set to the **bottom** of the curve. With the eye above or below, the meniscus lines up with the wrong mark: this is **parallax error**.</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Titre uncertainty.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Two readings: $2 \times 0.05\ \text{mL} = 0.10\ \text{mL}$.

$$
\frac{0.10\ \text{mL}}{23.50\ \text{mL}} \times 100\% = \textbf{0.43\%}
$$

(b) Make the titre **larger**, for example by using a more dilute titrant or a larger volume of the solution being titrated, so the same ±0.10 mL is a smaller fraction of it.

</Answer>
