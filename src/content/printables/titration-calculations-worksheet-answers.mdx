## A. Titres

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>**Titre**: volume delivered from the burette. **Aliquot**: an accurately measured portion of a solution (by pipette). **End point**: where the indicator changes colour. **Equivalence point**: where exactly the stoichiometric amount has been added. **Concordant titres**: accurate titres that agree closely (within 0.10 mL).</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Hydrochloric acid.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Mean of the concordant titres (rough left out):

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bar{V} = \frac{(22.60 + 22.70 + 22.65)\ \text{mL}}{3} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{22.65 mL}
\end{aligned}
$$

Deviations −0.05, +0.05, 0.00 mL: $s = \sqrt{\dfrac{0.0050\ \text{mL}^2}{2}} = \textbf{0.05 mL}$

(b) $n(\ce{NaOH}) = 0.1050\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.02265\ \text{L} = 2.378 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} = n(\ce{HCl})$

$c(\ce{HCl}) = \dfrac{2.378 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.02500\ \text{L}} = \textbf{0.09513 mol/L}$

</Answer>

## B. Standardisation

<Q n={3}>Sodium carbonate.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $n = \dfrac{1.325\ \text{g}}{105.99\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.01250\ \text{mol}$; $c = \dfrac{0.01250\ \text{mol}}{0.2500\ \text{L}} = \textbf{0.05000 mol/L}$

(b) Aliquot: $0.05000\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.02500\ \text{L} = 1.250 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol Na}_2\text{CO}_3$; HCl $= 2 \times 1.250 \times 10^{-3} = 2.500 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

$c(\ce{HCl}) = \dfrac{2.500 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.02480\ \text{L}} = \textbf{0.1008 mol/L}$

</Answer>

## C. Diprotic acids and dilutions

<Q n={5}>Sulfuric acid.</Q>
<Answer>$n(\ce{NaOH}) = 0.2000\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.01825\ \text{L} = 3.650 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$; $n(\ce{H2SO4}) = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 3.650 \times 10^{-3} = 1.825 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$; $c = \dfrac{1.825 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.02500\ \text{L}} = \textbf{0.07300 mol/L}$</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Vinegar.</Q>
<Answer>

Aliquot: $0.1000\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.02330\ \text{L} = 2.330 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

Flask: $\dfrac{250.0\ \text{mL}}{25.00\ \text{mL}} = 10$ aliquots, so $2.330 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}$, all from the 20.00 mL of vinegar.

$c = \dfrac{2.330 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}}{0.02000\ \text{L}} = \textbf{1.165 mol/L}$

</Answer>

## D. Back titration

<Q n={7}>Antacid.</Q>
<Answer>

HCl added: $0.5000\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.02500\ \text{L} = 1.250 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{mol}$

HCl left over (= NaOH): $0.2500\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.01120\ \text{L} = 2.800 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

HCl reacted: $1.250 \times 10^{-2} - 2.800 \times 10^{-3} = 9.700 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

Mg(OH)₂ (1 : 2): $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 9.700 \times 10^{-3} = 4.850 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$; mass $= 4.850 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol} \times 58.32\ \text{g/mol} = 0.2829\ \text{g}$

Percent: $\dfrac{0.2829\ \text{g}}{0.300\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = \textbf{94.3\%}$

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Uncertainty.</Q>
<Answer>Two readings: $2 \times 0.05\ \text{mL} = 0.10\ \text{mL}$; $\dfrac{0.10\ \text{mL}}{22.65\ \text{mL}} \times 100\% = \textbf{0.44\%}$. To reduce it, make the titre **larger** (for example, use a more dilute titrant or a larger aliquot), so the same ±0.10 mL is a smaller fraction of the titre.</Answer>
