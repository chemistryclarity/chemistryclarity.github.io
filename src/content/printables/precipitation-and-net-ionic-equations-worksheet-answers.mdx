## B. Predicting precipitates

<Q n={2}>Precipitates.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Yes**: calcium carbonate, $\ce{CaCO3}$ (white). (b) **No**: KNO₃ and NaOH are both soluble. (c) **Yes**: copper(II) hydroxide, $\ce{Cu(OH)2}$ (blue). (d) **Yes**: silver iodide, $\ce{AgI}$ (yellow).</Answer>

## C. Writing equations

<Q n={3}>Barium chloride and sodium sulfate.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) -> BaSO4(s) + 2NaCl(aq)}$

(b)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\ce{Ba^2+(aq) + 2Cl-(aq) + 2Na+(aq) + SO4^2-(aq)} \\[4pt]
&\ce{-> BaSO4(s) + 2Na+(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)}
\end{aligned}
$$

(c) $\ce{Ba^2+(aq) + SO4^2-(aq) -> BaSO4(s)}$

(d) $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Net ionic equations.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\ce{Ca^2+(aq) + CO3^2-(aq) -> CaCO3(s)}$

(c) $\ce{Cu^2+(aq) + 2OH-(aq) -> Cu(OH)2(s)}$

(d) $\ce{Ag+(aq) + I-(aq) -> AgI(s)}$

</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Neutralization.</Q>
<Answer>$\ce{H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)}$. Strong acids and strong bases are fully ionized, so the anion of the acid and the cation of the base are always **spectators**: the only real change is H⁺ + OH⁻ forming water.</Answer>

## D. Calculations

<Q n={6}>Sulfate concentration.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&n(\ce{BaSO4}) = \frac{0.466\ \text{g}}{233.39\ \text{g/mol}} \\[4pt]
&= 1.997 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

1 mol $\ce{SO4^2-}$ gives 1 mol $\ce{BaSO4}$, so $n(\ce{SO4^2-}) = 1.997 \times 10^{-3}$ mol.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&c = \frac{1.997 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}}{0.0500\ \text{L}} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{0.0399 mol/L}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Lead(II) iodide.</Q>
<Answer>

$n(\ce{I-}) = 0.120\ \text{mol/L} \times 0.0200\ \text{L} = 2.40 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$

$\ce{Pb^2+ + 2I- -> PbI2}$: 2 mol $\ce{I-}$ give 1 mol $\ce{PbI2}$, so $n(\ce{PbI2}) = 1.20 \times 10^{-3}$ mol.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&m = 1.20 \times 10^{-3}\ \cancel{\text{mol}} \times 461.0\ \frac{\text{g}}{\cancel{\text{mol}}} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{0.553 g}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Purity of sodium chloride.</Q>
<Answer>

(a)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&m(\text{Cl}) = 0.717\ \text{g AgCl} \times \frac{35.45\ \text{g Cl}}{143.32\ \text{g AgCl}} \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{0.1773 g}
\end{aligned}
$$

(b)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\%\text{Cl} = \frac{0.1773\ \text{g}}{0.300\ \text{g}} \times 100\% \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{59.1\%}
\end{aligned}
$$

(c) Pure NaCl is $\dfrac{35.45\ \text{g/mol}}{58.44\ \text{g/mol}} \times 100\% = 60.66\%$ chlorine.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{purity} = \frac{59.12\%}{60.66\%} \times 100\% \\[4pt]
&= \textbf{97.5\%}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>
