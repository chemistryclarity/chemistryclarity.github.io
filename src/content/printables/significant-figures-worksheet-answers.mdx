## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Ruler.</Q>
<Answer>The millimetre marks make 4.3 cm certain, and you can **estimate one more digit** between the marks (4.37 cm). A fourth digit (4.372 cm) would be a guess beyond what the ruler can show.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Accuracy and precision.</Q>
<Answer>**Accuracy** is how close a result is to the true value; **precision** is how close repeated results are to each other. Precision describes the **spread of replicates**, so it needs at least two, and preferably three or more, measurements: one value has no spread to judge. Yes, results can be precise but not accurate: a balance that reads 0.20 g too high gives results that agree closely (small standard deviation) but are all wrong by the same amount (a systematic error).</Answer>

<Q n={9}>Triplicate weighing of a 2.500 g standard.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) Mean: sum = 2.52 g + 2.48 g + 2.51 g = 7.51 g; $\bar{x} = 7.51\ \text{g} \div 3 = 2.5033\ \text{g}$ (the 3 is an exact count).

Deviations: +0.0167 g, −0.0233 g, +0.0067 g. Squares: 2.778 × 10⁻⁴ g², 5.444 × 10⁻⁴ g², 0.444 × 10⁻⁴ g²; sum = 8.667 × 10⁻⁴ g².

$$
s = \sqrt{\frac{8.667 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{g}^2}{3 - 1}} = 0.0208\ \text{g} \approx 0.021\ \text{g}
$$

$\text{RSD} = \dfrac{0.0208\ \text{g}}{2.5033\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = 0.83\%$ (the units of g cancel)

(b) Error = 2.503 g − 2.500 g = **+0.003 g**; percent error = $\dfrac{+0.003\ \text{g}}{2.500\ \text{g}} \times 100\% =$ **+0.13%**

(c) The error (0.003 g) is much smaller than the standard deviation (0.021 g), so the result is **accurate**: there is no sign of a systematic error. The precision is moderate (RSD 0.8%); more replicates or a more precise balance would improve it. Result: **2.50 ± 0.02 g (n = 3)**.

</Answer>
