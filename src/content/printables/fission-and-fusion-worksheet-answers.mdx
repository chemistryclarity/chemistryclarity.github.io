## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>Definitions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Mass defect:** the mass of the separate protons and neutrons minus the mass of the nucleus. **Binding energy:** the energy needed to separate the nucleus into its nucleons, E = Δm c². (b) Binding energy per nucleon is **highest near iron-56**. Fission of a heavy nucleus and fusion of light nuclei both produce nuclei **higher on the curve** (more tightly bound); the increase in total binding energy is released.</Answer>

## C. Mass and energy

<Q n={3}>Helium-4.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\Delta m = 2(1.007276\ \text{u}) + 2(1.008665\ \text{u}) - 4.001506\ \text{u} =$ **0.030376 u**

$\Delta m = 0.030376\ \text{u} \times 1.66054 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg/u} =$ **5.044 × 10⁻²⁹ kg**

(b) $E = (5.044 \times 10^{-29}\ \text{kg})(2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s})^{2} =$ **4.534 × 10⁻¹² J**

(c) $\dfrac{4.534 \times 10^{-12}\ \text{J}}{4\ \text{nucleons}} =$ **1.13 × 10⁻¹² J per nucleon**

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>1.00 g of matter.</Q>
<Answer>

$E = (1.00 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{kg})(2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s})^{2} =$ **8.99 × 10¹³ J** (kg m²/s² = J)

</Answer>

## D. Fission and reactors

<Q n={5}>Reactor parts.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Moderator** (water or graphite): slows the neutrons so that uranium-235 captures them more easily. (b) **Control rods** (boron or cadmium): absorb neutrons; raising or lowering them controls the rate of fission. (c) **Coolant**: carries heat away from the core to make steam for the turbines. (d) **Critical mass**: the smallest mass of fissile fuel that can sustain a chain reaction.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Fission and fusion.</Q>
<Answer>Fission **splits heavy** nuclei, fusion **joins light** ones; fission produces **long-lived radioactive** waste, fusion mainly helium; fission is used in **power stations** today, fusion is **experimental**. Fusion needs very high temperatures because the nuclei are **positively charged and repel** each other: only very fast collisions bring them close enough to fuse.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Energy from uranium-235.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $200\ \text{MeV} \times 1.602 \times 10^{-13}\ \text{J/MeV} =$ **3.204 × 10⁻¹¹ J** per fission

(b) $3.204 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{J} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1} =$ **1.929 × 10¹³ J/mol**

(c) $\dfrac{1.929 \times 10^{13}\ \text{J/mol}}{235.04\ \text{g/mol}} =$ **8.21 × 10¹⁰ J/g**

(d) Methane: $\dfrac{890\ \text{kJ/mol}}{16.04\ \text{g/mol}} = 55.5\ \text{kJ/g} = 5.55 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/g}$; ratio $= \dfrac{8.21 \times 10^{10}\ \text{J/g}}{5.55 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/g}} \approx$ **1.48 × 10⁶** (about one and a half million times)

</Answer>

## E. Fusion and the Sun

<Q n={8}>D–D fusion.</Q>
<Answer>

$\Delta m = 2(2.014102\ \text{u}) - (3.016029\ \text{u} + 1.008665\ \text{u}) = 0.003510\ \text{u}$

$E = 0.003510\ \text{u} \times 931.5\ \text{MeV/u} =$ **3.27 MeV**

</Answer>

<Q n={9}>The Sun.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\Delta m = \dfrac{E}{c^{2}} = \dfrac{3.83 \times 10^{26}\ \text{J}}{(2.998 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s})^{2}} =$ **4.26 × 10⁹ kg** each second (J = kg m²/s², so J ÷ (m²/s²) = kg)

(b) 1.00 % of the mass: $0.0100 \times 1.989 \times 10^{30}\ \text{kg} = 1.989 \times 10^{28}\ \text{kg}$

$t = \dfrac{1.989 \times 10^{28}\ \text{kg}}{4.26 \times 10^{9}\ \text{kg/s}} = 4.67 \times 10^{18}\ \text{s}$

$4.67 \times 10^{18}\ \text{s} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{yr}}{3.156 \times 10^{7}\ \text{s}} =$ **1.48 × 10¹¹ yr**

</Answer>
