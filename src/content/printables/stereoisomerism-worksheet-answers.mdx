## A. Types of isomer

<Q n={1}>Isomer types.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **structural** (chain) isomers (b) **cis–trans** isomers (c) **structural** (position) isomers (d) the **same compound**: rotation about a C–C single bond is free.</Answer>

## B. Cis–trans and E/Z

<Q n={2}>Cis–trans?</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Yes**: one C has H and CH₃, the other H and C₂H₅. (b) **No**: one carbon has two CH₃ groups and the other two H atoms. (c) **Yes**: each carbon has H and Br.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>1,2-Dichloroethene.</Q>
<Answer>Cis: both Cl on the same side; trans: Cl on opposite sides. **Cis boils higher (60 °C)**: its two C–Cl dipoles add up, so the molecule is **polar** with dipole–dipole forces. In the trans isomer the dipoles cancel, so it is non-polar (48 °C).</Answer>

<Q n={4}>E or Z.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Cl beats H and Br beats H; Cl and Br on the same side: **Z**. (b) On one carbon CH₃ (C, 6) beats H; on the other Cl (17) beats H; they are on opposite sides: **E**.</Answer>

## C. Optical isomerism

<Q n={5}>Chiral centres.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **C2** (H, Cl, CH₃, C₂H₅) (b) **none**: the central C has two CH₃ groups (c) **C2** (H, OH, CH₃, COOH) (d) **none**: every carbon has at least two H atoms.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Lactic acid enantiomers.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Same: formula, structure (connections), boiling point, melting point, density and most reactions. (b) Different: they rotate **plane-polarized light in opposite directions**, and they can react differently with other chiral molecules such as enzymes. A **racemic mixture** is a 50 : 50 mixture of the two enantiomers; it shows no overall rotation.</Answer>

## D. Applications

<Q n={7}>Single-enantiomer drugs.</Q>
<Answer>Drug targets (enzymes and receptors) are themselves chiral, so usually only one enantiomer **fits** the target. In a racemic mixture half the dose may be inactive, or may cause side effects. A single enantiomer can give the same effect at a lower dose with fewer side effects.</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Butanols.</Q>
<Answer>In **butan-2-ol** carbon 2 carries four different groups (H, OH, CH₃, C₂H₅), so it is a **chiral centre** and there are two enantiomers. In **butan-1-ol** no carbon has four different groups (C1 carries two H atoms), so there are no stereoisomers. Butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol differ in the position of the OH group: they are **structural (position) isomers**.</Answer>
