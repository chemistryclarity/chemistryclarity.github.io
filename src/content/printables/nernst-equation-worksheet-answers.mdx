## A. Ideas

<Q n={1}>Battery voltage.</Q>
<Answer>As the cell runs, reactants are used up and products build up, so the reaction quotient **Q increases**. By the Nernst equation, $E = E° - \frac{0.0592\ \text{V}}{n}\log Q$, so E **decreases**. When the reaction reaches equilibrium, Q = K and **E = 0**: the battery is flat.</Answer>

## B. Using the Nernst equation

<Q n={2}>Zn–Cu cell.</Q>
<Answer>

$E° = 1.10$ V, $n = 2$, $Q = \dfrac{0.10}{2.0} = 0.050$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = 1.10\ \text{V} - \frac{0.0592\ \text{V}}{2}\log 0.050 \\[4pt]
&= 1.10\ \text{V} + 0.0385\ \text{V} = \textbf{1.14 V}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Larger** than E°: Q is less than 1 (more reactant $\ce{Cu^2+}$ than product $\ce{Zn^2+}$), so the reaction has a greater driving force.

</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Ag–Cu cell.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $E° = 0.80\ \text{V} - 0.34\ \text{V} = \textbf{0.46 V}$; $n = \textbf{2}$ (2 Ag⁺ each gain 1 e⁻)

(b) $Q = \dfrac{[\ce{Cu^2+}]}{[\ce{Ag+}]^2} = \dfrac{1.0}{(0.010)^2} = 1.0 \times 10^{4}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&E = 0.46\ \text{V} - \frac{0.0592\ \text{V}}{2}\log(1.0 \times 10^{4}) \\[4pt]
&= 0.46\ \text{V} - 0.118\ \text{V} = \textbf{0.34 V}
\end{aligned}
$$

</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Silver concentration cell.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) The **dilute** side (0.0010 M) is the anode: Ag is oxidized there, raising its $\ce{Ag+}$ concentration.

(b) $E° = 0$, $n = 1$, $Q = \dfrac{0.0010}{0.10} = 0.010$; $E = 0 - 0.0592\ \text{V} \times \log 0.010 = \textbf{+0.118 V}$

</Answer>

## C. E°, ΔG° and K

<Q n={5}>ΔG° and K.</Q>
<Answer>

(a) $\Delta G° = -2 \times 96\,485\ \text{C/mol} \times 0.46\ \text{V} = -8.88 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J/mol} = \textbf{−88.8 kJ/mol}$

(b) $\log K = \dfrac{2 \times 0.46\ \text{V}}{0.0592\ \text{V}} = 15.54$, so $K = \boldsymbol{3.5 \times 10^{15}}$

</Answer>

<Q n={6}>E° from K.</Q>
<Answer>$E° = \dfrac{0.0592\ \text{V}}{1}\log(1.0 \times 10^{5}) = 0.0592\ \text{V} \times 5.0 = \textbf{0.30 V}$</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Negative E°.</Q>
<Answer>$\Delta G° = -nFE°$ is **positive**, and K is **less than 1**: the reaction is not spontaneous under standard conditions, and reactants are favoured at equilibrium.</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>When E = 1.00 V.</Q>
<Answer>

$$
\begin{aligned}
&1.00\ \text{V} = 1.10\ \text{V} - \frac{0.0592\ \text{V}}{2}\log Q \\[4pt]
&\log Q = \frac{2 \times 0.10\ \text{V}}{0.0592\ \text{V}} = 3.38 \\[4pt]
&Q = \frac{[\ce{Zn^2+}]}{[\ce{Cu^2+}]} = \boldsymbol{2.4 \times 10^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The voltage falls only slowly at first: zinc ions must outnumber copper ions by about 2400 to 1 before E drops by 0.10 V.

</Answer>
