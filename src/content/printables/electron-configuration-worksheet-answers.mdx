## A. Understanding

<Q n={1}>p subshell.</Q>
<Answer>**3 orbitals**, each holding 2 electrons: **6 electrons** in total.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Pauli and Hund.</Q>
<Answer>**Pauli:** an orbital holds at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins. **Hund:** electrons in the same subshell occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before any orbital is doubly occupied.</Answer>

## B. Atoms

<Q n={3}>Full configurations.</Q>
<Answer>(a) C: $1s^2\, 2s^2\, 2p^2$ (b) Mg: $1s^2\, 2s^2\, 2p^6\, 3s^2$ (c) Cl: $1s^2\, 2s^2\, 2p^6\, 3s^2\, 3p^5$</Answer>

<Q n={4}>Noble-gas configurations.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Ca: **[Ar] 4s²** (b) Fe: **[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²** (c) Br: **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁵**</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Identify the elements.</Q>
<Answer>(a) 7 electrons: **nitrogen** (b) 10 + 7 = 17: **chlorine** (c) 18 + 14 = 32: **germanium**</Answer>

## C. Orbital diagrams

<Q n={6}>Nitrogen.</Q>
<Answer>2s: [↑↓] 2p: [↑] [↑] [↑]. By Hund's rule the three 2p electrons occupy separate orbitals: **3 unpaired electrons**.</Answer>

<Q n={7}>Unpaired electrons.</Q>
<Answer>(a) S, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴: 3p is [↑↓] [↑] [↑], so **2** (b) Ne, $1s^2\, 2s^2\, 2p^6$: all subshells full, so **0**</Answer>

## D. Ions

<Q n={8}>Ions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) $\ce{O^2-}$: 8 + 2 = 10 electrons, $1s^2\, 2s^2\, 2p^6$ = [Ne] (b) $\ce{Mg^2+}$: 12 − 2 = 10 electrons, [Ne] (c) $\ce{Fe^3+}$: remove both 4s electrons, then one 3d: **[Ar] 3d⁵**. **$\ce{O^2-}$ and $\ce{Mg^2+}$** have the same configuration (they are isoelectronic with neon).</Answer>

## E. Challenge

<Q n={9}>Chromium.</Q>
<Answer>A half-filled d subshell (one electron in each of the five 3d orbitals) is especially stable, so one 4s electron moves into 3d. Unpaired electrons: five in 3d plus one in 4s = **6**.</Answer>
