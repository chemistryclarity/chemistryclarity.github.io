## A. Structure

<Q n={1}>Structure of benzene.</Q>
<Answer>(a) Kekulé: a hexagon with alternating single and double bonds. Delocalized: a hexagon with a circle inside. (b) Each carbon is **sp²** hybridized, forming three σ bonds (two C–C and one C–H) in a flat ring, with 120° angles. Each carbon has one unhybridized **p orbital** perpendicular to the ring; the six p orbitals overlap side-on all round, so the six π electrons are **delocalized** above and below the ring.</Answer>

<Q n={2}>Evidence.</Q>
<Answer>Any two: **all C–C bonds are the same length** (139 pm, between 154 and 134 pm); the **enthalpy of hydrogenation** is much less exothermic than for three C=C bonds; benzene **does not decolourize bromine water** and reacts by **substitution** rather than addition.</Answer>

<Q n={3}>Empirical formula.</Q>
<Answer>

In 100 g: C 92.3 g, H 7.7 g.

$n(\text{C}) = \dfrac{92.3\ \text{g}}{12.01\ \text{g/mol}} = 7.69\ \text{mol}$; $n(\text{H}) = \dfrac{7.7\ \text{g}}{1.008\ \text{g/mol}} = 7.6\ \text{mol}$

Ratio 7.69 : 7.6 ≈ 1 : 1, so the empirical formula is **CH** (and the molecular formula C₆H₆ = 6 × CH).

</Answer>

## B. Stability and reactions

<Q n={4}>Delocalization energy.</Q>
<Answer>Expected for three C=C: $3 \times (-120\ \text{kJ/mol}) = -360\ \text{kJ/mol}$. Actual: $-208\ \text{kJ/mol}$. Difference: $-208 - (-360) = \textbf{152 kJ/mol}$. Benzene releases 152 kJ/mol less energy because it starts **152 kJ/mol lower in energy** (more stable) than a structure with three separate double bonds; the extra stability comes from delocalization.</Answer>

<Q n={5}>Reactions.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **Concentrated HNO₃ + concentrated H₂SO₄**, about **50 °C**: nitrobenzene, $\ce{C6H5NO2}$ (+ H₂O). (b) **Br₂** with a **FeBr₃** (or Fe) catalyst: bromobenzene, $\ce{C6H5Br}$ (+ HBr). Both are **electrophilic substitution**.</Answer>

<Q n={6}>Names.</Q>
<Answer>(a) **methylbenzene** (toluene) (b) **benzoic acid** (c) **chlorobenzene** (d) **phenol**</Answer>

## D. Challenge

<Q n={8}>Nitration of methylbenzene.</Q>
<Answer>

$n(\ce{C7H8}) = \dfrac{9.21\ \text{g}}{92.13\ \text{g/mol}} = 0.09997\ \text{mol}$ (1 : 1)

Theoretical: $0.09997\ \text{mol} \times 137.14\ \text{g/mol} = \textbf{13.7 g}$

Percent yield: $\dfrac{10.5\ \text{g}}{13.71\ \text{g}} \times 100\% = \textbf{76.6\%}$

</Answer>
